SUBJ: BOI ATCT/TRACON Standard Operating Procedures

  This document establishes the Boise Airport Traffic Control Tower (BOI ATCT) and Big Sky

Terminal Radar Approach Control (BOI TRACON) Standard Operating Procedures within the Salt Lake

City ARTCC on VATSIM (vZLC). Controllers are required to be familiar with the provisions of this

document and to exercise their best judgment if they encounter situations not covered by it. The

provisions and procedures described herein are supplemental to vZLC Facility Policies and FAA Order JO

7110.65.

  The information contained herein is to be used for flight simulation purposes only on the VATSIM

network. It is not intended, nor should it be used for real-world navigation. The Virtual Salt Lake City

ARTCC is not affiliated with the FAA, the actual Salt Lake City ARTCC, or any governing aviation body.

1-3-1. BOI Runway Configurations
    a. The active runway configuration refers to the general cardinal direction of departures and
         arrivals currently in use. Authorized runway configurations are listed in subparagraphs e.
         and f.
    b. When selecting a Runway Flow, the following factors must be considered:
              (1) Wind direction.
              (2) Wind speed. The gust factor takes precedence over the constant wind speed.
    c. When changing runway flows, the following items must be accomplished:
              (1) The radar position(s) must coordinate with ZLC ARTCC, in accordance with the
                   BOI-ZLC LOA.
              (2) All operational positions must be verbally advised of:
                        (a) Impending runway configuration changes.
                        (b) The callsign of the last departure and arrival in the old configuration.
              (3) Ensure the ATIS broadcast is updated.
    d. East Flow is designated as the "calm wind" runway configuration.
    e. East Flow: Landing and departing Runway 10L and Runway 10R.
    f. West Flow: Landing and departing Runway 28L and Runway 28R.

1-3-2. BOI Automatic Departure Releases
    a. BOI Local Control has automatic release for all departures.
    b. Sage/Alpine has control on contact for all departures.

1-3-3. BOI Departure Instructions
    a. IFR departures must be issued the following:
              (1) Runway heading.
              (2) An initial altitude of 16,000 feet, or lower filed altitude.

                   NOTE-
                   IFR departures must be assigned a minimum of 8,000 feet.

    b. Departures requesting VFR-on-top must not be restricted above 16,000 feet.

    b. DVAs must not be utilized when a SID or ODP has been assigned, or when issuing
         missed approach/go-around instructions.
    c. The following DVAs, listed in Table 1-3-1 and 1-3-2, are authorized:

1-3-5. Vectors Below the MVA
Air traffic is responsible for terrain and obstruction avoidance when vectoring an aircraft that is
not on a published procedure below the MVA/MIA. This is primarily performed for aircraft
conducting a go-around/missed approach. The following provisions must be complied with when
vectoring aircraft below the MVA:

    a. BOI:
              (1) Runway 10L/10R: Vectors below the MVA are not authorized.
              (2) Runway 28L/28R: Headings between 200° clockwise to 330° are authorized in
                   accordance with FAA JO 7110.65 paragraph 5-6-3. STARS Map 13 must be
                   depicted when vectoring below the MVA.

    b. BZN:
              (1) Runway 12: Headings between 245° clockwise to 302° are authorized.
              (2) Runway 30: Headings between 215° clockwise to 298° are authorized.

1-3-6. BOI Missed Approach/Go-Around Procedures
IFR aircraft executing an unplanned missed approach/go-around must comply with the following
procedures:

    a. IFR aircraft that are not on a visual approach must be instructed to fly the published
         missed approach procedure and to maintain 8,000 feet.

    b. IFR aircraft that are on a visual approach can be issued either of the following:
              (1) Instruct the pilot to fly runway heading and maintain 8,000 feet.

                   NOTE-
                   A heading between 200° clockwise to 330° may be given to aircraft on a visual
                   approach to Runway 28L/R. This heading must only be assigned after the aircraft
                   has crossed the runway threshold.

              (2) Instruct the pilot to join the local traffic pattern, in accordance with FAA JO
                   7110.65, paragraph 7-4-1.

1-3-7. BOI Visual Separation
    a. When Sage or Alpine is using pilot-applied visual separation on arrival traffic, "VS" must
         be placed in Scratchpad 2 of the aircraft's full data block, unless otherwise coordinated.
    b. When Local Control is using pilot-applied visual separation on successive departures,
         verbal coordination must be accomplished.

1-3-8. BOI Practice Approaches (Planned Missed Approaches)
    a. The following climb out instructions must be issued to aircraft requesting multiple
         approaches in Sage or Alpine airspace:
              (1) IFR: Alpine is responsible for issuing the following climb out instruction:

                   PHRASEOLOGY-
                   "Climb out instructions: fly the published missed approach, maintain eight
                   thousand."

              (2) VFR: Local Control is responsible for issuing instructions. Local Control may
                   issue any heading in accordance with Table 2-3-1 or Table 2-3-2, depending on
                   the runway flow.

    b. Alpine must ensure the appropriate practice approach information is entered into the
         data block, in accordance with the ZLC Scratchpad Directive (7210.4).

    c. Alpine must handoff the aircraft to Local Control, regardless of the aircraft's flight rules.
    d. Local Control must handoff the aircraft to the appropriate sector following the missed

         approach.

1-3-9. BOI Automated Point-Outs
The STARS automated point-out function may be used to point-out traffic between Local Control
and/or Sage/Alpine under the following conditions:

    a. The transferring controller must enter the destination or direction of flight in the
         scratchpad area of the STARS data block prior to initiating the automated point-out.

    b. The receiving controller must accept the point-out using automation to indicate no
         altitude or speed restrictions are imposed, or verbally coordinate altitude/speed/route
         restrictions prior to accepting the point-out.

             Section 4. Pre-Arranged Coordination Procedures (PACP)

1-4-1. General
Pre-arranged coordination refers to the procedure where one controller may penetrate another
controller's airspace without coordination, on the basis that the penetrating aircraft is operating
on a pre-determined track/route, which enables the controller applying PACP to determine
whether or not appropriate separation can be applied between the penetrating aircraft and other
traffic. When applying PACP, the following procedures must be complied with:

    a. Two positions of operation cannot be authorized to penetrate each other's airspace
         simultaneously.

    b. Any penetration of another controller's airspace that is not designated as a pre-arranged
         coordination area (see Appendix 2) is prohibited, unless otherwise coordinated.

    c. The following must be considered prior to applying PACP. If any of the following
         situations exist, PACP should not be used:
              (1) Complexity and volume of traffic in the pre-arranaged airspace.
              (2) Weather or other conditions that may disrupt the normal flow of traffic.
              (3) Whether or not the use of PACP will disrupt or adversely affect the normal flow of
                   traffic for the other sector.

    d. PACP may be terminated at any time by the controller responsible for the pre-arranged
         airspace and must not be resumed until additional coordination has been accomplished.

    e. The controller using PACP in another controller's airspace must:
              (1) Continuously display data block information for all traffic in that sector's airspace,
                   which must contain, at a minimum, the position symbol and altitude information.
              (2) Assume separation responsibility for, and avoid, all traffic under the jurisdiction of
                   the controller responsible for the pre-arranged airspace.

1-4-2. Application
    a. Sage departure aircraft may depart Local Control airspace through the areas depicted in
         Appendix 2.
    b. Alpine has control to turn VFR departures to the north. Any deviation from the Local
         Control assigned heading to the south must be coordinated with Sage.
    c. Arrival aircraft sequenced by Sage/Alpine to BOI airport must be routed through the
         arrival zones, as depicted in Appendix 3.

                    Section 5. Opposite Direction Operations (ODO)

1-5-1. General
Opposite Direction Operations consists of IFR/VFR operations conducted to the same or
parallel runway where an aircraft is operating in a reciprocal direction of another aircraft arriving,
departing, or conducting an approach.

1-5-2. Responsibilities
    a. Local Control and Sage/Alpine share the responsibility to coordinate ODO and issue
         traffic advisories (see below).
    b. Local Control is responsible for cutoff point compliance for opposite direction arriving and
         departing aircraft. STARS Map 710 (ODO Cutoff Points) must be displayed.
    c. Sage/Alpine is responsible for cutoff point compliance for opposite direction arrival/arrival
         aircraft. STARS Map 710 (ODO Cutoff Points) must be displayed.

1-5-3. IFR Aircraft Procedures
    a. General:
              (1) Traffic advisories must be issued to both aircraft conducting ODO.

                   PHRASEOLOGY-
                   OPPOSITE DIRECTION TRAFFIC (distance) MILE FINAL, (type aircraft).
                   OPPOSITE DIRECTION TRAFFIC DEPARTING RUNWAY (number), (type
                   aircraft).
                   OPPOSITE DIRECTION TRAFFIC (position), (type aircraft).

              (2) Opposite direction, same or parallel runway, operations with opposing traffic
                   inside the cutoff point are prohibited.

              (3) Visual separation is not authorized during ODO.
              (4) Aircraft conducting RNAV Z approaches must be cleared from OFTER or JIMMI

                   for ODO to be authorized.
    b. Coordination:

              (1) Local Control must verbally request opposite direction departures with
                   Sage/Alpine and include aircraft callsign, aircraft type, runway, and the phrase
                   "opposite direction."

              (2) Sage/Alpine must verbally request opposite direction arrivals with Local Control
                   and include aircraft callsign, aircraft type, runway, and the phrase "opposite
                   direction."

    c. Cutoff procedures for aircraft conducting ODO:
              (1) A departing aircraft, including an aircraft performing a touch-and-go, stop-and-go,
                   go-around, low approach, or missed approach, must be airborne and have
                   commenced a turn to avoid conflict prior to an aircraft reaching:
                        (a) A point 10 flying miles from the threshold of the runway of intended
                             landing, or;
                        (b) The base leg turn for an aircraft established in the traffic pattern.

Not for real world use.                    7
              (2) An arriving aircraft must cross the runway threshold prior to an aircraft reaching:
                        (a) A point 15 flying miles from the threshold of the runway of intended
                             landing, or;
                        (b) The base leg turn for an aircraft established in the traffic pattern.

              (3) If the above conditions are not met, action must be taken to ensure control
                   instructions are issued to protect the integrity of the cutoff points.

                   NOTE-
                   Flying miles are defined as the distance an aircraft is expected to fly (via pilot
                   navigation) or required to fly (via control instructions) to reach the landing
                   threshold of the runway of intended landing.

1-5-4. VFR Aircraft Procedures
    a. Ensure VFR aircraft are issued a turn to avoid conflict with the opposing IFR/VFR traffic.
    b. During coordination, the phrase "opposite direction" must be used.
    c. Traffic information must be issued to both aircraft, including the direction that the
         departure will turn (as appropriate) and/or the location of the opposing aircraft on final
         (as appropriate).

                           Section 1. Clearance Delivery (CD)

2-1-1. Position Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the CD frequency (125.900 MHz).
    b. Prepare and update flight progress strips in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip
         Handling Directive (7210.3).
    c. Issue departure clearances to IFR aircraft, complying with posted traffic management
         initiatives (TMIs), letters of agreement (LOAs), and published preferred routes.
    d. Issue departure clearances to VFR aircraft in accordance with paragraph 2-1-7.
    e. Create VFR flight plans for departing VFR aircraft.
    f. Assume other clearance delivery or other cab responsibilities, as assigned.

2-1-2. IFR Departure Routing

         given.
    b. All aircraft that will leave the Big Sky TRACON must be routed via one of the departure
         fixes listed in Table 2-1-1. Aircraft that are unable to be routed via a departure fix must
         be routed through a departure corridor or coordinated with ZLC ARTCC. Departure
         corridors are depicted and described in the BOI-ZLC LOA.

2-1-3. IFR Departure Altitudes
    a. All aircraft must be issued an initial departure altitude of 16,000 feet, or lower filed
         altitude.
    b. Aircraft with a filed altitude below 16,000 feet that will exit Big Sky TRACON airspace
         must be assigned an altitude at or above the minimum altitude for their departure fix, in
         accordance with Table 2-1-1.

2-1-4. Use of Pre-Departure Clearances (PDCs)
    a. Unless an operational necessity exists, a route amendment is required, or the aircraft is
         impacted by a posted TMI (except as outlined in paragraph 2-1-6), all PDC capable
         aircraft must be sent a PDC via vTDLS in lieu of a voice clearance.
    b. Fill out the boxes in vTDLS as follows:
              (1) "Expect" box: Always select "10 MIN AFT DP" unless the aircraft's initial climb
                   instruction is equal to their requested/assigned altitude. In this instance, leave the
                   selection in this box blank.
              (2) "SID" box: Always select "NO SID".
              (3) "Transition" box: Must always be left blank.
              (4) "Climb out" box: Always select "FLY RUNWAY HEADING".
              (5) "Climb via" box: Must always be left blank.
              (6) "Maintain" box: Select the initial altitude assignment in accordance with
                   paragraph 2-1-3.
              (7) "Contact info" box: This box should be left blank unless the aircraft will push back
                   into the movement area.
              (8) "Departure frequency" box: Select the appropriate departure frequency for the
                   aircraft's route of flight and/or current staffing. Temporary non-standard departure
                   frequencies can be added in the vTDLS Facility Menu.
              (9) "Local info" box: Must always be left blank.

2-1-5. Reroute Procedures
When a reroute is required, CD must proceed with the following steps in order:

    a. Dump the aircraft from vTDLS.
    b. Amend the flight plan as necessary in the flight plan window, complying with paragraph

         2-1-2.
    c. Print the amended flight progress strip, ensuring the revision number is correct, and

         delete the original strip.
    d. Verbally issue the departure clearance to the aircraft.
    e. Mark the amended flight progress strip in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip Handling

         Directive (7210.3).

2-1-6. Compliance with TMIs
    a. Aircraft affected by posted TMIs must be advised of any delays verbally.
    b. Aircraft affected by posted TMIs must have pertinent TMI information placed in their flight
         progress strip, in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip Handling Directive (7210.3).
    c. Aircraft affected by posted TMIs must not be issued a PDC.

                               Section 2. Ground Control (GC)

2-2-1. Position Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the GC frequency (121.700 MHz).
    b. Ensure all taxiing aircraft have the current ATIS.
    c. Determine/finalize aircraft departure runway assignments.
    d. Marks FPSs in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip Handling Directive (7210.3).
    e. Assume other ground control responsibilities, as assigned.
    f. Assume CD responsibilities when combined.

2-2-2. Area of Jurisdiction
GC has jurisdiction of all taxiways in the movement area, except for all taxiways located
between Runway 10L and Runway 10R.

NOTE-
Taxiway J between Runway 10R and Taxiway W must be considered located between the
runways, and therefore is not the jurisdiction of GC.

2-2-3. Traffic Flow
GC must ensure all aircraft yield to aircraft exiting a runway, unless otherwise coordinated with
Local Control.

2-2-4. ATIS
Ensure all departure aircraft have the current ATIS.

2-2-5. Multiple Runway Crossings
A waiver has been granted to BOI ATCT for FAA JO 7110.65, paragraph 3-7-2, which requires
an aircraft to have crossed a previous runway before another runway crossing clearance can be
issued. Thus, aircraft may be cleared to cross both runways at BOI with a single clearance.

2-2-6. Pushback Clearances
To the maximum extent possible, aircraft parked at the terminal ramp should push back within
the non-movement area.

2-2-7. Use Of SAID System
    a. SAID only displays ADS-B-equipped and transmitting aircraft/vehicles within the
         site-specific adapted area (the airport itself, not the surrounding area).
    b. SAID systems may rely solely on ADS-B transponder information to display aircraft
         identification if NAS flight plan information is not available to the system. This may result
         in a discrepancy between SAID depicted aircraft identification and NAS flight plan
         identification. This means that aircraft must have their ADS-B on with Altitude Reporting
         Enabled in order to move on the airport movement areas.
    c. SAID-derived information may only be used for reference and requires confirmation via
         visual observation and/or pilot/operator reporting.

                           Section 3. Local Control (LC)

2-3-1. Position Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the LC frequency (118.100 MHz).
    b. Provide control instructions to aircraft within the LC area of jurisdiction.
    c. Operate TDW/STARS equipment, as necessary.
    d. Marks FPSs in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip Handling Directive (7210.3).
    e. Assume other local control or other cab responsibilities, as assigned.

2-3-2. Area of Jurisdiction
LC has jurisdiction of the following:

    a. LC airspace, as depicted in Appendix 1A.
    b. Runway 10L/28R.
    c. Runway 10R/28L.
    d. All taxiways between Runway 10L/28R and Runway 10R/28L.

2-3-3. Use of Radar
When using TDW/STARS, LC must:

    a. Comply with the provisions contained within FAA JO 7110.65 Chapter 5.
    b. Adjust the range of the radar to display a minimum of 10 NM.
    c. Display the BOI Tower List.

2-3-4. Departure Procedures
For all departing aircraft, LC must:

    a. Provide initial separation between all departures.
    b. Assign all non-turbojet VFR departures a heading and altitude restriction in accordance

         with Table 2-3-1 in East Flow or Table 2-3-2 in West Flow. LC may assign any heading
         within the heading ranges described in the applicable table.

    c. Assign all turbojet VFR departures runway heading without an altitude restriction.
    d. Coordinate with Sage/Alpine if a departure aircraft does not auto-acquire within 3 NM of

         the departure end of the runway.
    e. Advise Sage/Alpine of aircraft executing an unplanned missed approach/go-around and

2-3-5. Arrival Procedures
For all arriving aircraft, LC must:

    a. Quick-look the Alpine sector.

         NOTE-
         When Alpine is combined to Sage, LC must quick-look the Sage sector.

    b. Sequence and separate VFR downwind traffic from all other traffic after accepting the
         handoff from Sage/Alpine.

    c. Have control of arrivals on initial contact. If LC issues control instructions that affect the
         sequence, LC assumes responsibility for separation and must coordinate with
         Sage/Alpine.

         NOTE-
         Coordination may be verbal or via STARS scratchpad.

    d. Issue traffic advisories to aircraft when:
              (1) Two aircraft are on opposing bases.
              (2) One aircraft is established on final and another aircraft will turn base to final
                   beside the aircraft established on final.

2-3-6. Landing Clearance
The use of a memory aid upon issuance of a landing clearance is mandatory. The preferred
method for utilizing a landing clearance memory aid is by highlighting the aircraft's STARS Full
Data Block (FDB).

2-3-7. Multiple Runway Crossings
A waiver has been granted to BOI ATCT for FAA JO 7110.65, paragraph 3-7-2, which requires

                    Chapter 3. TRACON Operational Positions

                                   Section 1. General Control

3-1-1. Facility Identification
When working TRACON sectors listed in sections 3-2 through 3-4, controllers must identify
themselves as "Big Sky Approach" or "Big Sky Departure."

3-1-2. Scratchpad Use
All scratchpad entries must be made in accordance with the ZLC Scratchpad Directive (7210.4).
At a minimum, all arrival aircraft must have the approach type and runway placed in Scratchpad
#1.

3-1-3. Altitude Filter Limits
Each position is required to set the filter limits at a minimum of 1,000 feet below the surface to
1,000 feet above the vertical limits assigned to that position. Other altitudes may be viewed at
the controller's discretion.

                                     Section 2. Alpine Radar

3-2-1. Position Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the Alpine frequency (126.900 MHz).
    b. Provide air traffic control services within the Alpine delegated airspace (see Appendix 2B
         and 2C).

3-2-2. LC Coordination
Alpine must:

    a. Sequence all arrivals to BOI airport except for VFR aircraft handed off to LC.
    b. Ensure separation of arrivals to the LC airspace lateral boundary, except when wake

         turbulence separation minima applies. In this instance, ensure separation to the runway
         threshold.
    c. Transfer communications of arrival traffic to LC prior to the LC airspace boundary, but no
         further than 10 NM from the airport.
    d. Hand off all VFR arrivals to LC prior to the LC airspace boundary.

3-2-3. Procedures
    a. Alpine must adjust the range of their radar to display a minimum of 11 NM outside
         delegated airspace.
    b. Aircraft handed off from Sage are released for turns and descent, except for arrivals in
         the southernmost downwind during East Flow.

                                   Section 4. Bozeman Radar

3-4-1. Position Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the Bozeman frequency (118.975 MHz).
    b. Provide air traffic control services within the Bozeman delegated airspace (see Appendix
         2D).

3-4-2. Procedures
Bozeman must:

    a. Adjust the range of the radar to display a minimum of 10 NM outside of delegated
         airspace.

    b. Coordinate with Bozeman Federal Contract Tower (FCT) to determine the approach in
         use to be broadcast on the ATIS.

    c. Coordinate with BZN FCT for aircraft that are cleared for any type of approach other than
         advertised.

    d. Provide applicable separation for VFR aircraft conducting practice instrument
         approaches.
