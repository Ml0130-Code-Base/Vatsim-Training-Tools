SUBJ: ZLC ARTCC Standard Operating Procedures

  This document establishes the Salt Lake City Air Route Traffic Control Center (ZLC ARTCC)

Standard Operating Procedures within the Salt Lake City ARTCC on VATSIM (vZLC). Controllers are

required to be familiar with the provisions of this document and to exercise their best judgment if they

encounter situations not covered by it. The provisions and procedures described herein are supplemental

to vZLC Facility Policy and FAA Order JO 7110.65.

  The information contained herein is to be used for flight simulation purposes only on the VATSIM

network. It is not intended, nor should it be used for real-world navigation. The Virtual Salt Lake City

ARTCC is not affiliated with the FAA, the actual Salt Lake City ARTCC, or any governing aviation body.

Cameron Negrete
Air Traffic Manager
Salt Lake City ARTCC

         Section 4. Assumption and Transfer of Position Responsibility

1-4-1. Duty Familiarization
Prior to assuming responsibility for any position, controllers must accomplish the following items:

    a. Review current weather, trends, and forecasts for the sector(s) they will assume
         responsibility for.

    b. Review all Traffic Management Programs.
    c. Review pertinent underlying and neighboring controller information.
    d. Log-in to position and activate session.
    e. If applicable, log-in to vStrips.
    f. If applicable, log-in to vTDLS.

1-4-2. Transfer of Position Responsibility
    a. All position relief briefings must be conducted in accordance with FAA Order 7110.65
         Appendix A.
    b. Controllers must activate the session of the position they are assuming prior to the start
         of the position relief briefing.
    c. When conducting a position relief briefing, all controllers must follow the Position Relief
         Checklist (POS CHECK) located in the "CHECK LISTS" toolbar menu of ERAM.
    d. When traffic conditions warrant, the controller being relieved must monitor and observe
         the position for a minimum of 2 minutes after the conclusion of the briefing.

1-4-3. Position Relief Checklist Description
Subparagraphs a. through g. are the checklist items listed in the Position Relief Checklist. The
clauses listed underneath each subparagraph are items that should be included in each position
relief briefing, as necessary.

    a. SECTORS
              (1) The configuration of the sector that is being transferred.
              (2) The status of the sectors adjacent to the position being transferred.

    b. EQUIPMENT / AUTO-TRK
              (1) The status of equipment and resources we use for controlling.
              (2) Auto-Track status.

    c. WEATHER
              (1) ATIS letters and status.
              (2) Lowest usable flight level.
              (3) PIREPs.
              (4) Deviations.
              (5) SIGMETs.

    d. AIRPORTS / SIA
              (1) Pertinent status of airports under the jurisdiction of the sector being transferred.
              (2) Any item in paragraph 1-4-1 that should be reviewed.

    e. MILITARY
              (1) The status of any military aircraft.

    f. TRAFFIC

                                     Section 5. Coordination

1-5-1. Facility Identification
A waiver to FAA JO 7110.65, paragraph 2-4-19, has been granted to ZLC ARTCC, which allows
for the use of "Salt Lake Center" as opposed to "Salt Lake City Center."

1-5-2. Transfer of Control
The completion of a radar handoff and transfer of communications between sectors constitutes
a transfer of control for all of the following:

    a. Climb/descent into the receiving controller's airspace, if applicable.
    b. Turns up to 15°.
    c. Speed adjustment ±10 knots.
    d. Beacon code change.

1-5-3. Entry of Assigned Altitude
When inputting an assigned altitude into an aircraft's data block, the provisions of FAA JO
7110.65, paragraph 5-13-3, must be adhered to, except during the handoff scenarios listed
below. During these scenarios, interim altitude entry is not required when a climbing aircraft is
assigned an altitude strictly based on sector stratification or standard operating procedure.

    a. Sector 11 handing off to Sector 44.
    b. Sector 30 handing off to Sector 41.
    c. Sector 31 handing off to Sector 41.

1-5-4. Fourth-Line Data
Intra-facility coordination using fourth-line data is authorized, provided the entries used are in
accordance with FAA JO 7110.65, paragraph 5-4-10. The following fourth-line data entries must
be considered valid coordination in addition to those entries listed in FAA JO 7110.65,
paragraph 5-4-10.

    a. Instrument Approaches: Pilot requests for, or clearances given for, instrument
         approaches must be put in the fourth-line of the aircraft's data block using the following
         format:
              (1) Approach type. Utilize Table 1-5-1 for the type of approach requested/cleared.
              (2) Approach rendition (i.e. "Z" or "Y"), if applicable.
              (3) Runway. Utilize "A" when a visual approach to a non-towered airport is being
                   conducted.
              (4) Approach termination, if other than full stop, following a hyphen.

    d. The phrase "PD" means the aircraft has been issued a descent at pilot's discretion to the
         assigned altitude in the data block.

1-5-5. Inappropriate Altitude for Direction of Flight (IAFDOF)
Aircraft whose route of flight course is within the ranges of 340°-020° or 160°-200° may be
assigned any legal altitude regardless of direction of flight. Any resulting IAFDOF altitudes need
not be coordinated.

NOTE-
This must only apply to intra-facility coordination, unless otherwise specified in a LOA.

                                      Section 6. Automation

1-6-1. 3nm Reduced Separation Areas
Reduced IFR separation of 3nm may be used in accordance with FAA JO 7110.65, paragraph
5-5-4, within the areas depicted in Appendix 2 at or below FL230.

NOTE-
Due to current ERAM limitations, all targets at or below FL230 will appear as a Reduced
Separation target. 3nm Reduced Separation is only authorized in the areas depicted in
Appendix 2.

1-6-2. Automated Information Transfer (AIT)
AIT is a process by which two controllers, whose sectors may not be adjacent, may transfer
radar identification and communications of an aircraft to each other via a third intermediary
controller, but without transferring communications to the intermediary controller. Except for
instances listed in subparagraphs b. and c., aircraft must be in level flight at their assigned
altitude throughout the AIT process. Should any AIT process be implemented, clauses (1)
through (3) must be adhered to sequentially.

    a. General Procedures:
              (1) Controller A must initiate transfer of radar identification to Controller B.
              (2) Controller B, after accepting the transfer of radar identification and traffic
                   permitting, must initiate a transfer of radar identification to Controller C before the
                   aircraft enters Controller B's airspace.
              (3) Once Controller A observes Controller C accept the transfer of radar
                   identification, Controller A must transfer aircraft communications to Controller C
                   before the aircraft enters Controller C's airspace.
              (4) If Controller A does not observe Controller B initiate a transfer of radar
                   identification to Controller C prior to the A/B sector boundary, Controller A must
                   transfer aircraft communications to Controller B.

                   NOTE-
                   Controller A may, at their discretion, transfer aircraft communications to
                   Controller B prior to Controller C accepting the transfer of radar identification.

    b. BZN/Sector 15 Procedures: This process may be used for Bozeman (BZN) arrivals
         17,000 feet and above that will enter the lateral confines of Sector 6 from Sector 15 and
         enter the Bozeman sector of Big Sky TRACON.
              (1) Sector 15 must clear the aircraft to 17,000 and then initiate a transfer of radar
                   identification to Sector 6.
              (2) Sector 6 must accept the transfer of radar identification and then initiate a
                   transfer of radar identification to Big Sky Approach.
              (3) Sector 15 must transfer communication of the aircraft to Big Sky Approach after
                   observing Big Sky Approach accept radar identification.

    c. BZN/Sector 19 Procedures: This process may be used for BZN arrivals 17,000 feet and

1-6-3. Target Markers (Data Block Retention)
When an aircraft is departing from an non-towered airport, or a towered airport that ZLC ARTCC
provides approach control services to, a track or departure message needs to be initiated as
soon as practical. There are two methods of accomplishing this:

    a. Track Method (preferred): The QT command (see below) will start a track over the
         aircraft's departing airport and issue a departure message automatically. The track will
         have a coasting status and remain over the departure airport until the aircraft is airborne
         and picked up by radar.

         QT //<ICAO Airport> <ACID>

    b. Departure Message Method: The DM command (see below) will issue a departure
         message, but will not initiate a track until the aircraft is airborne and picked up by radar.

         DM <ACID>

                                Section 7. Traffic Management

1-7-1. General
    a. In accordance with VATUSA DP003, all Traffic Management Initiatives (TMIs) posted to
         the National Traffic Management Log (NTML) by VATUSA Division Command Center
         (DCC) must be complied with.
    b. TMU procedures outlined in Chapter 6 must be complied with.

1-7-2. Salt Lake City Terminal Area
    a. When transferring aircraft to Salt Lake TRACON, the transferring controller must ensure
         each aircraft is on its own navigation via the appropriate STAR or coordinate a radar
         vector heading.
    b. Issue speed/altitude crossing restrictions to aircraft in accordance with the S56-ZLC
         LOA.

         EXAMPLE-
         "Cross SPANE at and maintain flight level one-niner-zero, at two-eight-zero knots."

    c. Issue expected runways in accordance with the S56-ZLC LOA to aircraft on RNAV
         arrivals.

         EXAMPLE-

                                         Section 1. General

6-1-1. TMU Qualification
To serve as TMU during an ARTCC-sanctioned event, a controller must have watched the ZLC
TMU video and must be designated as TMU by the vZLC Events Coordinator.

6-1-2. National Traffic Management Log (NTML) Use
The NTML must be used during events in accordance with the following procedures:

    a. Prior to an event starting, the CIC or EC must post an opening statement in the NTML,
         located within the vATCSCC Discord. It should include all of the following for featured
         airports:
              (1) Timestamp (formatted as <Day of Month>/<UTC Time>)
              (2) Airport (FAA 3-letter identifier)
              (3) Expected Weather Conditions (VMC, LVMC, IMC, and LIMC may be used. These
                   condition categories should be decided by looking at TAFs and AFDs)
              (4) Runway Configuration
              (5) AAR and ADR for Runway Configuration

         EXAMPLE-
         13/0043 SLC VMC ARR:34L/35 DEP:34R/35 AAR:62 ADR:62

    b. If traffic demand is expected to be high enough to warrant a Traffic Management
         Initiative (TMI) from neighboring facilities, they must be posted in the NTML and include
         all of the following:
              (1) Timestamp (formatted as <Day of Month>/<UTC Time>)
              (2) Airport (FAA 3-letter identifier)
              (3) Routing
              (4) Restriction
                        (a) The phrase "AS ONE" or "PER STREAM" must be included.
                        (b) Exclusions must be included.
              (5) Reason
              (6) Applicable Timeframe
              (7) Requesting and Restricted Facility

         EXAMPLE-
         13/2336 SLC via ALL 15MIT PER STREAM EXCL:NONE VOLUME:VOLUME
         2359-0400 ZLC:ZLA,ZOA,ZSE
         13/2336 SLC via HELPR 25MIT AS ONE EXCL:PROPS VOLUME:VOLUME
         2359-0400 ZLC:ZDV

    c. Any TMI cancellation must be posted in the NTML.

Not for real world use.                    91
         EXAMPLE-
         13/0249 SLC via ALL CANCEL TMI ZLC:ZDV,ZLA

6-1-3. Offload Procedures
In the event that gate balance begins to affect MIT restrictions for aircraft inbound to Salt Lake
City (SLC), the TMU or CIC may begin to offload SLC arrivals to different gates. Because of
topography, options may be limited at times. All reroute requests must be posted in the NTML.

    a. SLC Landing North:
              (1) QWENN# arrivals may be offloaded to the YUTES# or BVL# arrival. In this
                   instance, aircraft must be cleared direct BVL and routed via BVL.YUTES# or
                   BVL.BVL#, assuming the UTTR's are closed.
              (2) JAZZZ# or SPANE# arrivals routed via MTU may be offloaded to the CARTR# or
                   LHO# arrival. If this is going to be done, ZLC TMU must ask ZDV to reroute
                   aircraft via OCS.CARTR#/CKW.CARTR or OCS.LHO# as soon as practical. This
                   is not a recommended option due to the increase in flight length. Planes will not
                   be flying a downwind in addition to the reroute.

    b. SLC Landing South:
              (1) SKEES# or BEARR# and BCE.PITTT#, MLF.PITTT#, or JAMMN arrivals may be
                   offloaded to the YUTES# or BVL# arrival. In this instance, aircraft must be
                   cleared direct BVL and routed via BVL.YUTES# or BVL.BVL#, assuming the
                   UTTR's are closed.
              (2) CARTR# or LHO# arrivals routed via CKW may be offloaded to the MTU.PITTT#
                   or SPANE arrival. If this is going to be done, TMU must ask ZDV to reroute
                   aircraft via MTU.PITTT# or MTU.SPANE# as soon as practical. ZLC TMU may
                   also ask ZMP to reroute as well. This procedure can also be applied vice versa
                   (e.g. MTU.PITTT# offloaded to CKW.CARTR#).
