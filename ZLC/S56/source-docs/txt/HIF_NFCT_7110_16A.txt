SUBJ: HIF AFB Standard Operating Procedures

  This document establishes the Hill Air Force Base Non-Federal Contract Air Traffic Control Tower

(HIF NFCT) Standard Operating Procedures within the Salt Lake City ARTCC on VATSIM (vZLC). This

SOP establishes standardized air traffic control, flight operations, and airfield management procedures at

Hill Air Force Base (HAFB). It ensures safe, efficient, and coordinated air traffic operations by detailing

responsibilities, communication protocols, and operational guidelines. The provisions and procedures

described herein are supplemental to vZLC Facility Policies and FAA Order JO 7110.65.

  The information contained herein will be used only for flight simulation purposes on the VATSIM

network. It is not intended, nor should it be used for real-world navigation. The Virtual Salt Lake City

ARTCC is not affiliated with the FAA, DOD, Salt Lake City ARTCC, or any governing aviation body.

                    Section 3. Intrafacility Coordination Procedures

1-3-1. Runway Configurations
The active runway configuration refers to the general cardinal direction of departures and
arrivals currently in use. Unless otherwise coordinated with Salt Lake City TRACON (S56), the
active runway must be selected in accordance with the following:

              (1) When the tailwind is component 10 knots or greater for Runway 14, Runway 32
                   should be used.

              (2) When the tailwind component exceeds 10 knots for Runway 32 or the weather is
                   below circling minima, Runway 14 should be used.

    a. South Flow is designated as the "calm wind" runway configuration.
    b. South Flow: Landing and departing Runway 14.
    c. North Flow: Landing and departing Runway 32.

1-3-2. Changing Runway Configurations
The Local Control (LC) position is the final authority for selecting the runway configuration, but
coordination with GC and TRACON must be accomplished prior to changing the runway
configuration.

    a. LC must:
              (1) Coordinate with GC and S56 to determine which aircraft will be the last departure
                   and arrival in the old runway configuration.
              (2) Ensure that:
                        (a) The Tower Display Workstation (STARS) display is adjusted to indicate
                             the correct flow.
                        (b) The ATIS is updated to reflect the runway configuration change.

    b. S56 TRACON must:
              (1) Ensure the STARS display is adjusted to indicate the correct flow.
              (2) Notify S56 TRACON Hart And Bear Sectors (or other appropriate overlying
                   sector) of the runway configuration change.

1-3-3. ATIS
Regardless of the position broadcasting the ATIS, the following statements must be included on
the ATIS broadcast:

    a. During VFR weather conditions: "DEPARTING VFR AIRCRAFT ADVISE GROUND
         CONTROL OF REQUESTED HEADING OR DESTINATION."

    b. When the outside air temperature is 26°C or greater: "CHECK DENSITY ALTITUDE."

         NOTE-
         This statement must be made immediately after stating the altimeter setting.

                    Section 4. Opposite Direction Operations (ODO)

1-4-1. General
Opposite Direction Operations consist of IFR/VFR operations conducted to the same or parallel
runway where an aircraft is operating in a reciprocal direction of another aircraft arriving,
departing, or conducting an approach.

NOTE-
Departing aircraft include aircraft conducting a touch-and-go, stop-and-go, low approach,
missed approach, or go-around.

1-4-2. Responsibilities
    a. LC and Hart (S56) share the responsibility to coordinate ODO and issue traffic advisories
         (see below).
    b. LC is responsible for applying the cutoff point between opposite direction arriving and
         departing aircraft.
    c. Hart is responsible for applying the cutoff point between opposite direction arrival
         aircraft.

1-4-3. IFR Aircraft Procedures
    a. General:
              (1) Traffic advisories must be issued to all ODO aircraft.

                   PHRASEOLOGY-
                   OPPOSITE DIRECTION TRAFFIC (distance) MILE FINAL, (type aircraft).
                   OPPOSITE DIRECTION TRAFFIC DEPARTING RUNWAY (number), (type
                   aircraft).
                   OPPOSITE DIRECTION TRAFFIC (position), (type aircraft).

              (2) VFR aircraft conducting practice approaches must receive IFR services.
              (3) Opposite direction, same or parallel runway, operations with opposing traffic

                   inside the cutoff point are prohibited.
              (4) Lateral separation must exist before any other type of separation is applied.
    b. Coordination:
              (1) LC must verbally request opposite direction departures with S56.
              (2) S56 must verbally request opposite direction arrivals with LC.
              (3) Initial coordination must include callsign, type, and arrival/departure runway. The

                   phrase "opposite direction" must be used.
    c. Cutoff procedures for aircraft conducting ODO to the same runway:

NOTE-
When both aircraft are receiving IFR services, visual separation is not authorized.

Not for real world use.                    4
              (1) A departing aircraft must be airborne and have turned to avoid conflict prior to an
                   aircraft reaching:
                        (a) A point five flying miles from the threshold of the runway of intended
                             landing or, if an aircraft is established in the traffic pattern, prior to that
                             aircraft turning base leg.
                        (b) If the above conditions are not met, action must be taken to ensure
                             control instructions are issued to protect the integrity of the cutoff points.

              (2) An arriving aircraft must cross the runway threshold prior to an aircraft reaching:
                        (a) A point five flying miles from the threshold of the runway of intended
                             landing or, if an aircraft is established in the traffic pattern, prior to that
                             aircraft turning base leg.
                        (b) If the above conditions are not met, action must be taken to ensure
                             control instructions are issued to protect the integrity of the cutoff points.

    d. Cutoff procedures for aircraft conducting ODO to parallel runways:
              (1) Provide a turn away from the opposing arriving traffic before the arrival is inside
                   of the cutoff point to the other runway. The same cutoff points in subparagraph c.
                   must apply.

NOTE-
1. Visual separation of any type may be applied after the turn away from opposing traffic is
issued.
2. Prior to a pilot providing visual separation, a heading with at least 15° angular difference
between the aircraft's flight paths must be assigned to ensure flight paths do not cross.

1-4-4. VFR Aircraft With ODO Operations In Effect Procedures
    a. Ensure VFR aircraft are issued a turn to avoid conflict with the opposing IFR/VFR traffic.
    b. During coordination, the phrase "opposite direction" must be used.
    c. LC must issue traffic information to both aircraft and indicate the direction that the
         departure will turn (arrival/departure ODO) or the location of the opposing aircraft on final
         (arrival/arrival ODO).

                      Chapter 2: Tower Operational Positions

                                 Section 1. Clearance Delivery

2-1-1. Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the CD frequency (124.100/335.80 MHz).
    b. Prepare and update flight progress strips in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip
         Handling Directive (7210.3).
    c. Issue departure clearances to IFR aircraft in accordance with posted traffic management
         initiatives (TMIs), letters of agreement (LOAs), and published preferred routes.
    d. Issue departure clearances to VFR aircraft.
    e. Assume other clearance delivery or other cab responsibilities, as assigned.

2-1-2. IFR Departure Routing
IFR departure aircraft must be routed in accordance with the following procedures:

    a. Except when the flight crew indicates they are unable to accept, all aircraft must be
         assigned the following SIDs depending on the departure runway:
              (1) Runway 14: FRMNT#. The DEVLN# may be issued if filed by the aircraft.
              (2) Runway 32: WLLRD#. The DEVLN# may be issued if filed by the aircraft.

    b. If the flight crew is unable to accept a SID, the aircraft must be instructed to fly the
         following headings, depending on the runway in use:
              (1) Runway 14: 290°
              (2) Runway 32: Runway heading.

2-1-3. IFR Departure Altitudes
    a. IFR departure aircraft cleared via the DEVLN# SID must be instructed to maintain 9,000
         feet and expect filed/amended altitude 10 minutes after departure.
    b. IFR departure aircraft not cleared via the DEVLN# SID must be instructed to maintain
         the following altitudes, dependent on the runway in use, and expect filed altitude 10
         minutes after departure:
              (1) Runway 14: 6,500 feet.
              (2) Runway 32: 7,000 feet.

2-1-4. VFR Departure Instructions
    a. VFR departure aircraft must be handled in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip Handling
         Directive (7210.3).
    b. Unless otherwise coordinated with LC, VFR departure aircraft requesting any direction of
         flight other than westbound must be instructed to expect one of the following VFR
         transitions/departures:
              (1) Mountain Road Transition (Northbound or Southbound).
              (2) I-15 Transition (Northbound or Southbound).
              (3) MUDFLAT Transition (Westbound)

                                   Section 2. Ground Control

2-2-1. Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the GC frequency (121.600/275.80 MHz).
    b. Ensure all taxiing aircraft have the current ATIS.
    c. Mark FPSs in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip Handling Directive (7210.3).
    d. Assume other ground control responsibilities, as assigned.
    e. Assume CD responsibilities when combined.

2-2-2. Area of Jurisdiction
GC has jurisdiction of all taxiways in the movement area.

2-2-3. Traffic Flow
GC must ensure all aircraft yield to aircraft exiting a runway, unless otherwise coordinated with
LC.

2-2-4. ATIS
GC must ensure all departing aircraft have the current ATIS information prior to communications
transfer to LC.

2-2-5. Procedures
    a. Hill AFB assigned aircraft and tow vehicles are authorized to taxi and tow on their
         respective ramps, including USFS on the Alert Ramp and other parking areas approved
         by AMOPS (i.e., hot pads) without prior ATC permission. ATC permission must be
         received before taxi or tow operations outside controlled ramp areas.
    b. Aircraft requiring de-arm shall establish and maintain contact with GC before taxiing from
         the de-arm area. The Tower establishes preferred taxi routing depending on ground
         traffic.
    c. Heavy aircraft are requested to taxi with outboard engines off or idle when possible to
         reduce FOD potential.

2-2-6. Departure
    a. Control all taxiing aircraft except those on active runways.
    b. Approve pushback and start-up clearances.
    c. Ensure aircraft request taxi clearance with position and ATIS confirmation.

2-2-7. Arrivals
    a. Aircraft not requiring de-arming after landing shall establish and maintain contact with
         GC before entering Taxiway Alpha.

                                     Section 3. Local Control

2-3-1. Responsibilities
    a. Monitor the LC frequency (127.150/263.15 MHz).
    b. Provide control instructions to aircraft within the LC area of jurisdiction.
    c. Operate TDW/STARS equipment, as necessary.
    d. Marks FPSs in accordance with the ZLC Flight Strip Handling Directive (7210.3).
    e. Assume other local control or other cab responsibilities, as assigned.

2-3-2. Area of Jurisdiction
LC has jurisdiction of the following:

    a. LC airspace, as depicted in Appendix 1.
    b. Runway 14/32.

2-3-3. Runway Selection
Unless otherwise coordinated with Salt Lake City TRACON (S56), the active runway must be
selected in accordance with the following:

    a. When the tailwind component is 10 knots or less, or the weather is below circling
         minima, Runway 14 should be used.

    b. When the tailwind component exceeds 10 knots from the north, Runway 32 should be
         used.

2-3-4. Use of Radar
When using TDW/STARS, LC must:

    a. Comply with the provisions contained within FAA JO 7110.65 Chapter 5.
    b. Adjust the range of the radar to display a minimum of 15 NM.
    c. Display the HIF Tower List.

2-3-5. Departure Procedures
    a. A "Release Request" (.RR) is required to be sent to the appropriate departure controller
         for all departing IFR aircraft.
    b. Departing aircraft must not climb above 6,300 feet until past the departure end of the
         runway when another aircraft is conducting the overhead maneuver.
    c. Departing VFR aircraft that will remain outside of the Ogden Class D airspace (see
         Appendix 1):
              (1) Must be restricted at or below 7,500 feet until:
                        (a) Clear of the Hill Class D airspace (see Appendix 1), or;
                        (b) Communications transfer to S56.
              (2) May be instructed to depart on course.
    d. Departing northbound VFR aircraft that will penetrate the Ogden Class D airspace must
         be restricted below 7,800 feet and communications transferred to Ogden LC (or other
         appropriate overlying controller).

Not for real world use.                    8
2-3-6. Arrival Procedures
    a. IFR arrivals inbound on an IAP to Runway 14 utilize the Hill Arrival Corridor (see
         Appendix 1B). The Hill Arrival Corridor must not be utilized for any other operation,
         except as described in 5-8.
    b. VFR arrivals should enter the Hill Class D on a prescribed VFR transition, or over the
         Causeway (VPCWY) at 7,500 feet, to enter the traffic pattern.
    c. VFR arrivals or Category I/II IFR arrivals conducting a visual approach that execute a
         missed approach/go-around must be instructed to remain in the local traffic pattern, if
         capable of doing so.
    d. Except as described in subparagraph b., IFR arrivals that execute a missed
         approach/go-around must be instructed to comply with one of the following
         procedures/climbouts, depending on the arrival runway and organization status:
              (1) Aircraft associated with the Virtual United States Air Force (vUSAF):
                        (a) Runway 14: Issue Layton climbout.The Layton climbout requires aircraft
                             to "Track heading 139° until HIF 0.8 DME. Turn right heading 290, remain
                             within HIF 5.7 DME until passing a heading greater than 256°. Cross HIF
                             0.8 DME at or below 6,300 feet. Climb and maintain 6,500 feet."
                        (b) Runway 32: Issue Riverdale climbout. The Riverdale climbout requires
                             aircraft to "Track heading 319, cross HIF 1.6 DME at or below 6,300.
                             Climb and maintain 7,000."

PHRASEOLOGY-
"TALON 14, execute Layton climbout."

              (2) Aircraft not associated with the vUSAF:
                        (a) Runway 14: "Fly heading 290, climb and maintain 6,500."
                        (b) Runway 32: "Fly runway heading, climb and maintain 7,000."

    e. For aircraft that execute a missed approach/go-around that will not remain in the local
         traffic pattern, LC must:
              (1) Send a Missed Notification (.MA) to the appropriate S56 sector that will be
                   receiving the aircraft for re-sequencing.
              (2) Ensure the aircraft does not climb above 6,300 feet until past the departure end
                   of the runway when another aircraft is conducting the overhead maneuver.

2-3-7. Traffic Pattern Procedures
    a. Runway 14:
              (1) Category I aircraft: Right closed traffic at 5,800 feet.
              (2) Non-Fighter Category II/III aircraft: Right closed traffic at 6,300 feet.
              (3) Fighter aircraft: Left or Right closed traffic (LC's discretion) at 6,800 feet.
    b. Runway 32:
              (1) Category I aircraft: Left closed traffic at 5,800 feet.
              (2) Non-Fighter Category II/III aircraft: Left closed traffic at 6,300 feet.
              (3) Fighter aircraft: Left or Right closed traffic (LC's discretion) at 6,800 feet.

Right breaks are standard for aircraft conducting the overhead maneuver on Runway 32.

2-3-8. Additional vUSAF Procedures
To the maximum extent possible, LC must understand the following procedures for aircraft
associated with the vUSAF:

    a. VFR traffic re-entering the traffic pattern following a missed approach/go-around must
         execute the following procedure depending on their arrival runway:
              (1) Runway 14:
                        (a) Fly runway heading until the departure end of the runway, then make a
                             climbing left turn (towards the mountains) to 6,800 feet and enter a left
                             downwind.
              (2) Runway 32:
                        (a) Fly runway heading to the HIF 2.0 DME, then make a right turn, crossing
                             I-15 above 6,300 feet.
                        (b) The fighter will then turn north around the Freeport Center and continue
                             so their base turn places them 1 NM south of the Ogden Airport (KOGD).
                        (c) Altitude and airspeed will normally be 6,800 feet and 300 knots,
                             respectively.

NOTE-
This procedure is depicted in Appendix 2A.

    b. MUDFLAT recovery:
              (1) Runway 14:
                        (a) Maintain 7,300 feet until past KOGD, then descend to pattern altitude
                             (6,800 feet). Expect left turns.
              (2) Runway 32:
                        (a) Proceed to the downwind leg (3 NM west of the runway) at 7,500 feet until
                             abeam the runway threshold, then enter the overhead maneuver at 6,800
                             feet with right tums.

2-3-8. VFR Procedures
    a. VFR Missed Approach:
             i. To RE-ENTER the Hill traffic pattern from a MISSED APPROACH, the aircraft will
                   fly runway heading to the Hill TACAN 2 DME, then make a right turn crossing
                   1-15 above 6,300 'MSL
            ii. The fighter will then turn north around the Freeport Center and continue so his
                   base turn places him 1 NM south of the Ogden Airport.
            iii. Altitude and airspeed will be 6,800'MSL and 300 KIAS, respectively.
    b. VFR Departure Frequencies
             i. Handoff to departure control as soon as clear of any possible conflicts
                        1. S56 - 121.1 OR 135.5
            ii. If it's a north VFR departure under 7,800 handoff to Ogden FCT

    c. VFR Entering Pattern

             i. Enter Class D on prescribed VFR transition to enter traffic pattern to airport at
                   7,500 off of Causeway 4.

    d. VFR Flight Following
             i. Ensure all aircraft requiring flight following have the appropriate scratch pad entry
                   prior to handoff to departure.

    e. VFR Overhead Pattern
             i. VMC to expedite recovery and landing of fighter-type aircraft The pattern consists
                   of two 180-degree turns and resembles a race track
            ii. It begins at approximately 3 to 5 NM from the approach end of the runway at an
                   altitude of 6800' MSL
            iii. Normal airspeed for flying initial is 300 KIAS with speed decreasing through the
                   turns
            iv. Over the approach end of the runway the pilot makes a 180 degree turn to
                   downwind, then makes another 180 degree turn to complete the base to final
                   portion of the pattern
            v. The pattern is concluded either by a full stop or missed approach

2-3-9. VFR Traffic Pattern Runway 32:
    a. Light Civil A/C Use Left Closed Pattern @ 5,800
    b. Larger Than Fighter Type Aircraft Will Use Left Closed Pattern @ 6,300
    c. For Fighter Type Aircraft:
             i. Traffic Pattern Alt: 6,800
            ii. Left-Hand Or Right-Hand Circuits On All Runways (Tower's Choice)
    d. Right brakes are standard for Runway 32.
    e. The aircraft executing the MUDFLAT recovery will initially proceed to the downwind leg 3
         miles west of the runway at 7,500 until it abeams the landing threshold, then enter the
         overhead pattern at 6,800 right tums.
    f. Fly over Freeport center on the outside downwind
             i. Left reentries: Aircraft will enter an outside downwind 3NM west of the field at
                   6,800 and report a 3-mile initial.

2-3-10. VFR Traffic Pattern Runway 14:
    a. Light Civil A/C Use Right Closed Pattern @ 5,800
    b. Larger Than Fighter Type Aircraft Will Use Right Closed Pattern @ 6,300
    c. For Fighter Type Aircraft:
             i. Traffic Pattern Alt: 6,800
            ii. Left-Hand Or Right-Hand Circuits On All Runways (Tower's Choice).
    d. The aircraft executing the mudflat recovery will initially maintain 7,300 until past Ogden,
         then descend to pattern altitude 6,800 left turns.
    e. To remain in a CLOSED PATTERN, after a low/missed approach, the pilot will continue
         to the end of the runway and make a climbing left turn (towards the mountains) to an
         altitude of 6800' MSL.
