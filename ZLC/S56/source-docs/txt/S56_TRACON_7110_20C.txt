SUBJ: S56 TRACON Standard Operating Procedures

  This document establishes the Salt Lake City Terminal Radar Approach Control (S56 TRACON)

Standard Operating Procedures within the Salt Lake City ARTCC on VATSIM (vZLC). Controllers are

required to be familiar with the provisions of this document and to exercise their best judgment if they

encounter situations not covered by it. The provisions and procedures described herein are supplemental

to vZLC Facility Policy and FAA Order JO 7110.65.

  The information contained herein is to be used for flight simulation purposes only on the VATSIM

network. It is not intended, nor should it be used for real-world navigation. The Virtual Salt Lake City

ARTCC is not affiliated with the FAA, the actual Salt Lake City ARTCC, or any governing aviation body.

1-3-1. Definitions
    a. Altitudes are in feet mean sea level (MSL) except when describing cloud ceilings, which
         are in feet above airport elevation (AAE).
    b. Runway Flow: The primary direction of landing and departing traffic.
    c. North Flow: Operations at Salt Lake City International Airport (SLC) utilizing Runways
         34L, 34R, 35, and 32.
    d. South Flow: Operations at SLC utilizing Runways 16L, 16R, 17, and 14.

1-3-2. S56 TRACON Airspace
S56 TRACON airspace is depicted in Appendix 1. The airspace is divided into specific positions
of operation depicted in Appendix 3.

1-3-3. VFR Airspace
All operational positions are assigned the 500-foot altitude below their designated airspace
altitude except where such airspace overlies Alpine, Valley, and SLC ATCT airspace.

EXCEPTION-
Hart is assigned the 500-foot altitude above HS3 and HN4. See Appendix 3H.

                               Chapter 2. General Control

                                         Section 1. General

2-1-1. Intra-facility Transfer of Control
An intra-facility radar handoff and communications transfer constitutes transfer of control for:

    a. 30° turns either side of course.
    b. Climbs/Descents.
    c. Speed control ±30 knots.

2-1-2. Compliance with TMIs
All controllers must comply with all speed/in-trail restrictions, as mandated by the vATCSCC.

2-1-3. General Arrival Procedures
    a. On initial contact, the responsible radar position must:
              (1) Ensure receipt of airport arrival information (ATIS, AWOS, or ASOS).
              (2) Update the scratchpad in accordance with section 2-3. The scratchpad must
                   reflect the aircraft destination and applicable approach information.
    b. All SLC arrival aircraft that have been vectored off of an RNAV arrival must be
         re-established on the arrival prior to entering the lateral confines of Final airspace. This
         must be done in accordance with paragraphs 2-5-3 and/or 2-5-4.

2-1-4. General Departure Procedures
Controllers must not use the phrase "climb via SID" when there is conflicting crossing traffic.

    Section 2. Duty Familiarization and Transfer of Position Responsibility

2-2-1. Duty Familiarization
Prior to requesting a position relief briefing, controllers must accomplish the following items:

    a. Log-in to position and activate session.
    b. Review current METAR, PIREPs, or any other pertinent weather information.
    c. Review all Traffic Management Programs.
    d. Review pertinent overlying/underlying controller information.

2-2-2. Transfer of Position Responsibility
    a. All position relief briefings must be conducted in accordance with FAA Order 7110.65
         Appendix A.
    b. All controllers must follow the Position Relief Briefing Checklist located below.
    c. Controllers must activate the session of the position they are assuming prior to the start
         of the position relief briefing.
    d. When traffic conditions warrant, the controller being relieved must monitor and observe
         the position for a minimum of 2 minutes after the conclusion of the briefing.

                             Section 3. Automation

2-3-1. Use of Scratchpads
The use of scratchpads varies based upon whether the aircraft is IFR or VFR.

    a. IFR Aircraft:
              (1) The primary scratchpad will automatically populate with the following:
                        (a) The exit fix for departures, or;
                        (b) The three-letter airport identifier for arrivals.
              (2) The primary scratchpad may be used for the following:
                        (a) Speed assignments.
                        (b) Visual separation.
                        (c) Aircraft requesting a specific approach/runway.
                        (d) Other approved entries, in accordance with paragraph 2-3-2.
              (3) The secondary scratchpad must be used for the following:
                        (a) Approach assignment, in accordance with paragraphs 2-3-3 and 2-3-4.

    b. VFR Aircraft:
              (1) The primary scratchpad may be used for the following:
                        (a) Altitude restrictions.
                        (b) Practice approach information.
                        (c) Requested/expected runway.
                        (d) Other approved entries, in accordance with paragraph 2-3-5.
              (2) The secondary scratchpad must not be used.

                                      Section 4. Approaches

2-4-1. Simultaneous Independent Approaches to Widely Spaced Parallel Runways
(SIMULs)
SIMULs are authorized to Runway 16R and 17 or Runway 34L and 35 in accordance with FAA
Order 7110.65 5-9-10. The Runway 16R/34L and 17/35 centerlines are at least 9,200 feet apart.
The following procedures must be adhered to:

    a. Landing Runways 16R and 17:
              (1) Bear must sequence to Runway 17 and clear aircraft for the ILS approach prior to
                   UDUZU at or above 11,000 feet.

                   NOTE-
                   Bear must maintain control of aircraft until the SLC ATCT border. See paragraph
                   2-5-5.

              (2) Final must sequence to Runway 16R. Aircraft must be established on final and
                   cleared for the approach no farther south than RRUFF at or below 9,000 feet, or
                   the Wide Simul Line at or below 8,000 feet.

    b. Landing Runways 34L and 35:
              (1) Jordan must sequence to Runway 35 and clear aircraft for the LDA approach
                   prior to HLMET at or above 11,000 feet.

                   NOTE-
                   Jordan must maintain control of aircraft until the SLC ATCT border. See
                   paragraph 2-5-6.

              (2) Final must sequence to Runway 34L. Aircraft must turn base 4NM or more
                   outside of PUTER at or below 10,000 feet, or 3NM or more outside of CAMRI at
                   or below 9,000 feet.

    c. Final must assume responsibility for separation from Bear/Jordan traffic.

2-4-2. Simultaneous Dependent Approaches (Staggers)
Staggers are authorized to Runway 16L and 16R or Runway 34L and 34R in accordance with
FAA Order 7110.65 5-9-6. The Runway 16L/34R and 16R/34L centerlines are approximately
6,155 feet apart. The following procedures must be adhered to:

    a. Final must advise Bear/Jordan of the in-trail spacing needed for staggers.
    b. In South Flow, Bear must:

              (1) Sequence all arrivals onto the Runway 16L localizer.
              (2) Assign 11,000 feet and 210 knots.
              (3) Handoff to Final prior to IRRON.

    c. In North Flow, Jordan must:

2-4-3. Visual Approach Procedures
    a. When visual approaches are in use, the following runway pairings may be utilized:
              (1) Runway 16L and 16R.
              (2) Runway 16L and 17.
              (3) Runway 16R and 17.
              (4) Runway 34L and 34R.
              (5) Runway 34L and 35.
              (6) Runway 34R and 35.
    b. During North Flow (any runway pairing):
              (1) Final must sequence to the west runway and ensure that aircraft are at or below
                   9,000 feet prior to aircraft beginning the base turn.
              (2) Jordan must sequence to the east runway and ensure that aircraft are cleared for
                   the approach prior to entering the P4 Area.
    c. During South Flow (any runway pairing):
              (1) Final must sequence to the west runway and ensure that aircraft are at or below
                   8,000 feet prior to aircraft beginning the base turn.
              (2) Bear must sequence to the east runway and ensure that aircraft are cleared for
                   the approach prior to entering the P3 Area.
    d. Final must assume responsibility for separation from Bear/Jordan traffic.
    e. When clearing an aircraft for a visual approach to Runway 35 and separating from
         Runway 34R traffic, use the following phraseology:

         PHRASEOLOGY-
         "(Follow, fly, or track) the localizer to WEEEL, cleared visual approach runway
         three-five."

         OR

         "(Follow, fly, or track) the final approach course to WEEEL, cleared visual approach
         runway three-five."

                           Section 5. Pre-Arranged Coordination

2-5-1. Definition
Pre-arranged coordination is a process by which one controller allows an aircraft to penetrate or
transit another controller's airspace in a manner which ensures standard separation without
individual coordination for each aircraft.

2-5-2. Requirements
    a. Two positions of operation cannot penetrate each other's airspace simultaneously.
    b. Controllers who penetrate another controller's airspace must determine whether the lead
         aircraft requires wake turbulence separation behind it.

2-5-3. P1 Area (North Flow Only)
    a. Pre-arranged coordination is authorized during North Flow within the airspace depicted
         in Appendix 2A for the following sectors' departure aircraft:
              (1) Gunnison (encompassing portions of Bear and Lake airspace).
              (2) Stockton (encompassing portions of Final, Bear, Gunnison, and Lake airspace).
    b. When pre-arranged coordination is being utilized within the P1 Area, the following
         restrictions must exist:
              (1) Stockton must not turn SLC departure aircraft right of the course/route assigned
                   by the SLC ATCT Local Control (LC) position.
              (2) Gunnison departures off of SLC may climb and turn in the absence of Bear, Lake,
                   and Stockton traffic. Otherwise, Gunnison must assume responsibility for
                   separation.
              (3) Bear must establish arrivals into the Runway 34L downwind at 11,000 feet.
              (4) Stockton must establish arrivals into the Runway 34L downwind at 12,000 feet.

                Section 6. Off-Load Procedures and Responsibilities

2-6-1. Definitions
    a. SOP Runway: Refers to the standard runway that arrivals will land on at SLC. Downwind
         traffic is sent to the west runway and straight-in traffic is sent to the center or east
         runway.
    b. Runway Use Plan (RUP): As defined in the S56-SLC LOA.

2-6-2. Categories
Reasons for off-loading SLC arrivals to a runway other than the SOP runway fall into one of two
categories listed below:

    a. Arrival is off-loaded for the purpose of maintaining separation or the safety/integrity of
         the sector.

         EXAMPLE-
         The downwind is 20 miles long and the Final controller is struggling to keep up with
         demand. 1 or more aircraft are off-loaded to the center or east runway.

    b. Arrival aircraft prefers to land on a runway other than the SOP runway.

         EXAMPLE-
         N12345 is inbound via the downwind but prefers to land on the east runway, so they are
         off-loaded.

         NOTE-
         Verbal coordination with SLC ATCT is required if off-loading to the departure-only
         runway during a Departure-Only RUP.

2-6-3. Routing
Off-load arrivals from the downwind must be routed in the following manner:

    a. RNAV aircraft must fly the STAR into the downwind at 12,000 and 210 knots.
    b. Non-RNAV aircraft must be assigned a vector into the downwind (in accordance with

         paragraph 2-5-3 or 2-5-4) at 12,000 and 210 knots.

2-6-4. Automated Point-Outs
Non-verbal (automated) coordination may be utilized for approval of requested off-load runway
between Final and Bear or Jordan sectors, provided the following procedures are adhered to:

    a. Verbal coordination must be used if automated point-outs are not practical.
    b. The initiating controller must ensure that the primary scratchpad indicates the requested

         runway/approach prior to initiating an automated point-out. See paragraph 2-3-2.
    c. The receiving controller must verbally coordinate any required restrictions prior to

         accepting the point-out.
    d. After an automated point-out is approved, any subsequent changes must be verbally

         coordinated.
