1-3-1. Duty Familiarization
Prior to requesting a position relief briefing, controllers must accomplish the following items:

    a. L og-in to position and activate session.
    b. Log-in to vStrips.
    c. If applicable, log-in to vTDLS.
    d. R eview current METAR.
    e . Review all Traffic Management Programs.
    f. Review pertinent overlying controller information.

    a . All position relief briefings must be conducted in accordance with FAA Order 7110.65

    b. Controllers must activate the session of the position they are assuming prior to the start
         o f the position relief briefing.
    c. W hen traffic conditions warrant, the controller being relieved must monitor and observe
         the position for a minimum of 2 minutes after the conclusion of the briefing.

              S ection 5. Runway Flows and Runway Use Plans (RUP)

1-5-1. General
    a . T he active Runway Flow refers to the general cardinal direction of departures and
         arrivals currently in use. Authorized Runway Flows are listed in paragraph 1-5-3.
    b. T he active Runway Use Plan refers to the designation of traffic usage on individual
         runways in use. Authorized RUPs are listed below in paragraphs 1-5-5 and 1-5-6.
    c . RUPs must be selected in accordance with the active Runway Flow.
    d. R UPs must be selected by the CIC. If the CIC position has not been appointed, this duty
         is passed to the highest of the following operational positions:
              (1) The least (current or anticipated) busy Local Control.
              (2) T he least busy Ground Control.
    e . W hen selecting a Runway Flow, the following factors must be considered:
              (1) W ind direction.
              (2) Wind speed. The gust factor takes precedence over the constant wind speed.
    f. W hen selecting a RUP, the following factors must be considered:
              (1) Current and anticipated traffic volume.
              (2) C urrent and anticipated type of traffic (departures and/or arrivals).
              (3) Runway status.

1 -5-2. Flow/RUP Change Procedures
W hen changing runway flows or the Runway Use Plan, the CIC (or other appointed position)
must use the Runway Flow/RUP Change Checklist (below) and ensure the following:

    a . A ll operational positions acknowledge the new runway flow and/or RUP.
    b. All appropriate positions are informed of:

              (1) C allsign of the last departure in the old flow/RUP.
              (2) C allsign of the last arrival in the old flow/RUP.
              (3) N ew type of approaches in progress.
              (4) Automatic departure release status.

1 -5-4. RUP Naming
RUPs are coded/named in accordance with the following:

    a . For Normal Runways RUPs:
              (1) T he first two characters are "NR"
              (2) The third and fourth (if applicable) character(s) denote the departure plan (see

    b. For Departure-Only RUPs:
              (1) The first two characters are "XX"
              (2) T he third character denotes the runway identifier that is not available for arrivals.
              (3) The fourth and fifth (if applicable) character(s) denote the departure plan in use.

1-5-5. Normal Runways (NRI, NRV, NRV+, and NRVN)
During Normal Runways RUPs, all runways are available for arrivals and departures.

1-5-6. Departure-Only
When excessive departure demand and light arrival demand exist, one of the following RUPs
should be used:

    a . XXRI, XXRV, or XXRV+:
              (1) A ll runways are available for departures.
              (2) All runways except Runway 34R are available for arrivals.

    b . X XLI, XXLV, or XXLV+:
              (1) All runways are available for departures.
              (2) A ll runways except Runway 16L are available for arrivals.

    c . XX7I, XX7V, or XX7V+:
              (1) All runways are available for departures.
              (2) All runways except Runway 17 are available for arrivals.

    d . XX5I, XX5V, XX5V+, or XX5VN:
              (1) All runways are available for departures.
              (2) A ll runways except Runway 35 are available for arrivals.

                            S ection 6. Low Visibility Operations

1-6-1. General
    a . Unless otherwise determined in accordance with paragraph 1-6-2, low visibility
         operations must be in effect when any controlling RVR decreases to any RVR target
         v alue (see below) or a target value appears imminent.
              (1) L ow visibility operations begin when any controlling RVR decreases to less than
                   1 ,200 or a value of less than 1,200 appears imminent.
              (2) M ore restrictive procedures are initiated when a controlling RVR decreases to
                   less than 600.
    b. The ASDE-X must be used when low visibility operations are in progress.

1-6-2. Determination
When any controlling RVR decreases to an RVR target value or an RVR target value appears
imminent, the CIC (or other appointed position) should consider the extent of the RVR
o bscuration before commencing any low visibility operational configuration changes (i.e. is it an
isolated patch of ground fog or part of a larger weather system/trend).

1-6-3. Configuration Change Procedures
When commencing low visibility operations, the CIC (or other appointed position) must ensure
the following:

    a . All operational positions acknowledge the configuration changes.
    b. The phrase "SMGCS LOW VISIBILITY PROCEDURES ARE IN EFFECT" is included on

         the ATIS broadcast.
    c . T he ASDE-X is properly configured.
    d . All non-low visibility taxi routes and runways are clear of traffic.

1-6-4. Taxiway Usage
When low visibility procedures are in effect, the following taxiway restrictions exist:

    a. RVR 1,200 feet to 600 feet:
              (1) Taxiway E must only be used for eastbound traffic.
              (2) Taxiway F must only be used for westbound traffic.
              (3) T axiways A2, A3, A6, A9, A10, H6, H7, H12, and J must not be used.

    b . RVR 500 feet or less:
              (1) All restrictions listed in paragraph 1-6-4.a.
              (2) T axiways H2, H3, H11, K4, K6, K7, K8, P, N, and R must not be used.

2 -2-4. Use of Pre-Departure Clearances (PDCs)
    a. U nless an operational necessity exists, a route amendment is required, or the aircraft is
         impacted by a posted TMI (except as outlined in paragraph 2-2-7), all PDC capable
         a ircraft must be sent a PDC via vTDLS in lieu of a voice clearance.
    b. Fill out the boxes in vTDLS as follows:
              (1) "Expect" box: Always select "10 MIN AFT DP" unless the aircraft's initial climb
                   instruction is equal to their requested/assigned altitude. In this instance, leave the
                   s election in this box blank.
              (2) "SID" and "Transition" boxes: Ensure both boxes match the assigned SID and
                   transition.
              (3) "Climb out" box: "MAINTAIN 240 KTS UNTIL ADVISED" must be selected for

              (4) "Climb via" box: Must always be left blank.
              (5) "Maintain" box: Select the initial altitude assignment in accordance with
                   paragraph 2-2-2.
              (6) "Contact info" box: Make an applicable selection that conforms to current staffing
                   a nd special procedures. This box should be left blank unless gate hold
                   procedures are in effect or the aircraft will push back into the movement area.
              (7) "Departure frequency" box: Select the appropriate departure frequency for the
                   aircraft's assigned DP. Temporary non-standard departure frequencies can be
                   added in the vTDLS Facility Menu.
              (8) "Local info" box: Make a selection that matches the current flow and RUP.

         n ot equipped with CPDLC must be issued the amended departure clearance verbally.
         Ensure compliance with paragraphs 2-2-1 and 2-2-2.

              (1) T o send an amended departure clearance via CPDLC, select the aircraft in the
                   Messages Window and type the following command depending on the aircraft
                   type:
                        (a) For turbojet aircraft:.cpdlc [Departure Handoff ID]
                             [Departure runway]

2-2-6. Gate Hold Procedures
G ate hold procedures may be used to hold aircraft at the gate or other ground location
w henever departure delays exceed or are anticipated to exceed 15 minutes after engine start
a nd taxi time. When gate hold procedures are in effect, the following must apply:

    a. A ircraft must be advised to call CD prior to pushback.
              (1) For aircraft being sent a PDC, the following statements must be used in the
                   "Contact info" box of vTDLS:
                        (a) Aircraft parked within the terminal area or cargo ramps: "CTC 127.3
                             PRIOR TO PB"
                        (b) Aircraft parked elsewhere: "CTC 127.3 PRIOR TO ENG START"
