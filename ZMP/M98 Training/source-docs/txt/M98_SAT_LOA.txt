SUBJECT: TERMINAL OPERATIONS

1. PURPOSE. To prescribe procedures for the control of IFR and VFR air traffic at Anoka,
    Flying Cloud, Crystal, St. Paul Airports.

2. CANCELLATION. Initial Release.
3. SCOPE. This agreement between Minneapolis Terminal Radar Approach Control (M98) and

    its outlying Satellite (ANE, FCM, MIC, STP) ATCTs establishes responsibilities and
    procedures for handling IFR and VFR air traffic at the ANE, FCM, MIC, and STP Airports.
    These procedures are supplementary to FAA Order JO 7110.65, Air Traffic Control.
4. DEFINITIONS. Hereafter certain terms in this LOA shall be defined by abbreviations.

ATCT or ATCTs shall be defined as ANE, FCM, MIC, and STP ATCTs.

5. RESPONSIBILITIES.

    a. M98 must be responsible for providing approach control services to IFR arrivals,
         departures, and practice instrument approaches to ATCT airports in accordance with
         procedures contained herein.

    b. ATCTs must be responsible for designated IFR and SVFR operations in accordance with
         the procedures contained herein.

    c. Deviations from the procedures contained herein must be approved only after
         coordination between the facilities is effected which specifically defines responsibility
         and intent.

6. PROCEDURES.

    a. M98 must:

         1) Departures.

             (a) Issue departure releases via the Run-Down Tab List. Verbally coordinate
                  nonstandard headings and altitudes. (Standard headings and keyboard entries are
                  listed in Attachment 2.)

   2) Arrivals.
       (a) Ensure that full data blocks for arriving traffic contain the following data, unless
            verbal coordination with the appropriate ATCT is effected:
            1. Scratch pad entry data as listed in Attachment 1.
            2. Type aircraft in field four.

   3) Missed Approaches.
       (a) Issue IFR aircraft planning a missed approach the following instructions:

                ANE: Heading 360° and 2,500 ft.
                FCM: Heading 230° and 2,500 ft.
                MIC: Heading 320° and 2,500 ft.
                STP: Heading 060° and 2,500 ft.
            (b) Advise ATCTs of heading and altitude assigned to IFR and VFR aircraft
                executing a missed approach if different from (a) above.

       (c) Place aircraft in Run-Down Tab List, in the order they will depart, using STARS
            entries listed in Attachment 2.

       (d) Verbally coordinate any changes that have been made in the Run-Down Tab List
            whether or not the flash has been recalled.

       (e) Verbally coordinate all departures from non-advertised runways and any non-
            standard heading and/or altitude.

   2) Arrivals

       (a) FCM may change an aircraft's runway (side-step or circle) to any non-opposite
            direction runway without coordination. FCM assumes separation responsibility
            with any preceding and succeeding aircraft.

       (b) STP is authorized to interchange Runway 31 with 32 or Runway 13 with 14 when
            issuing landing instructions.

   3) Missed Approaches.

       (a) ATCTs must instruct any IFR aircraft executing an unplanned missed approach to
            fly the following headings and altitudes:

            ANE: Heading 360° and maintain 2,500 ft.

            FCM: Heading 230° and maintain 2,500 ft.

            MIC: Heading 320° and maintain 3,000 ft.

            STP: Heading 060° and maintain 2,500 ft.

       (b) Advise M98 when an IFR aircraft executes an unplanned missed approach.

                     Attachment 1: Scratch Pad Entries

   1) Scratch Pad 1 must have airport of intended landing for IFR or VFR aircraft.

            Example-
            STP, FCM, ANE, MIC, LVN, 21D, SGS

   2) Scratch Pad 2

   a) 1st character indicates type of instrument approach for IFR and VFR aircraft.
   b) 2nd/3rd character(s) indicates how the approach will terminate.

   c) VV ­ Aircraft has been cleared for a visual approach, and the pilot has assumed
       responsibility for separation (including wake turbulence separation) from the
       preceding aircraft. (Note: the scratchpad will look like VV+, the + is just an
       indication that the scratchpad is time-sharing).

   d) The following approach abbreviations must be entered in scratch pad 2:

            1st Character ­ TYPE of 2nd and 3rd Character ­ Full Stop or

            APPROACH                            Missed Approach

            I = ILS                             F = Full Stop

            G = RNAV (GPS)                      M = Missed Approach

            L = Localizer                       PM = Published Missed

            N = NDB                             VV = Pilot provided visual separation

            V = VOR

   Example ­ N12345 requesting ILS 30 to a Published Missed at LVN: STARS entry will

   be <+IPM, slew, enter>.
