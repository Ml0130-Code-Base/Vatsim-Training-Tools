# ZMP Training Toolset

Single-file, offline-first HTML training tools for a VATSIM virtual air traffic controller pursuing **ZMP (Minneapolis ARTCC) en route** certification. Same owner, same trainee, same nightly-practice purpose as the M98 toolset in `../M98 Training/` — **but a different job.** M98 is a terminal facility working a metroplex on STARS; ZMP is an en route facility working 33 sectors across six states on ERAM. The tools share an architecture and a house voice; they must not share operational data. Treat correctness of aviation data as safety-critical for training value: never invent procedures, fixes, altitudes, or paragraph citations — every operational number must trace to a reference file in this repo.

> **Skeleton status, 2026-09-01.** This folder was created as a *structural* skeleton alongside `../R90/`. The reference layer below is populated from the real source documents pulled the same day and is citable. **The tool itself (`zmp-drill-deck.html`) is a working shell, not a finished engine** — it carries the verified sector spine and the page/mount architecture, and the traffic model is deliberately empty. See *Roadmap*. **There are no ZMP training notes yet**: no OJT sessions, no practice log, no owner-observed shortfalls. `STANDING` is therefore empty and `claude_ZMP_OJT_Session_Review.md` is an intake template, not a review. Nothing in the tool may present a document-derived guess as an observed weakness.

## Verified operational data (2026-09-01)

Every number in this section is transcribed from a document in `source-docs/` or from the vNAS record, with the citation attached. Nothing here is inferred.

- **The governing order is ZMP 7200.1O CHG 2, effective 2024-11-15**, which cancels vZMP 7200.1N CHG 1 dated 2023-07-01 (7200.1O 1.4, 1.5). It is 135 pages and its per-sector chapters are the closest thing ZMP has to M98's SOP 7110.26A.
- **ZMP is 33 sectors in 6 Areas** (7200.1O table of contents, Chapters 4–9): **Area 1** — 01, 02, 03, 04, 12, 13 · **Area 2** — 05, 06, 10, 11, 15, 16, 21 · **Area 3** — 07, 08, 09, 17, 18, 19 · **Area 4** — 20, 22, 23, 24, 25, 33 · **Area 5** — 26, 29, 30, 36, 37 · **Area 6** — 27, 38, 39. Full table with frequency, stratum, scope range and combining rule in `claude_ZMP_Sector_Reference.md`.
- **Every sector frequency was checked against two independent sources** — the 7200.1O sector pages (`§x.y.3.1 Frequency and Sign-on Information`) and the vNAS facility record — and **they do not fully agree. 29 of the 33 match; four conflict.** **Sector 13** — SOP 133.45, vNAS 133.175. **Sector 24** — SOP 124.2, vNAS 132.15. **Sectors 22 and 25 are exactly transposed** — SOP has 22 on 134.55 and 25 on 134.75, vNAS has them the other way round. **The conflict is named, not resolved**: vNAS is the more recent record (2026-08-26 against the order's 2024-11-15) and it is what the client actually tunes, which is an argument and not a proof. `claude_ZMP_Sector_Reference.md` carries both columns for all four and the tool must not silently pick one. Do not quote a frequency for 13, 22, 24 or 25 in a drill without saying which source it came from.
- **vNAS carries ten ERAM sectors the SOP never staffs** — 14, 28, 32, 34, 40, 42, 43, 44, 46 and 83. They are adapted in the facility record but have no sector page, no area assignment and no procedures in 7200.1O. **The tool works the 33 documented sectors**; the other ten are recorded in the sector reference as adapted-but-undocumented and are not selectable as a seat.
- **Stratum is read off the ERAM altitude filter, not guessed.** `000B242` is a low sector (surface–FL230 band), `228B999` is a high sector, `000B999` is a sector that owns the full stratum (23, 24, 25), and Sector 21's `068B242` is the one sector with a hard floor. Source: the `Unique Sector Equipment Configurations` paragraph on each sector page.
- **ERAM altitude filters carry a deliberate buffer (7200.1O 3.2):** enter **1,200 ft** above and below the airspace limits when 1,000 ft vertical separation is in use, **2,200 ft** when 2,000 ft separation is in use. This is a ZMP-local rule and it is the first thing a trainee sets on the scope.
- **Separation is 5 NM across virtually all of vZMP (7200.1O 3.6).** 3 NM is authorised **only** inside the single-site areas depicted in Appendix 02, which exist because of the **Empire MI (QJA), Eagle River WI (EGV), Alpena MI (APN) and Sawyer MI (SAW)** radar sites. Two sector narratives give the radius in words — **Sector 01 within 54 miles of the APN site** and **Sector 02 within 40 miles of the Empire site**, both below FL230. Controllers must revert to 5 NM immediately on any single-site data anomaly. **Appendix 02 itself is a picture and the shaded areas are UNREAD** — the two radii above are the only ones written down.
- **Automated Information Transfer (7200.1O 3.7)** transfers altitude control, radar identification and fourth-line control information **without verbal coordination**, via the full data block during a radar handoff. ERAM only; radar-identified aircraft only; verbal coordination always overrides it; **it does not apply to point-outs** (7110.65 5-4-7). Authorised in all of vZMP regardless of stratum and with the first sector of an adjacent VATUSA facility — **VATCAN sectors cannot accept AIT**, which is why Toronto (YYZ) handoffs are manual.
- **Data Block Coordination (7200.1O 3.8)** is the heavier cousin: transfer of radar identification *plus* approval for climb/descent or interim altitude, **between two adjacent ZMP sectors only**. Conditions are all-or-nothing: aircraft **more than 5 minutes** from the boundary when the altitude is entered; handoff initiated **before 3 minutes** from the boundary; radar identified with valid Mode C; **never for departures within 5 minutes** of the boundary; verbal overrides. Acceptance of the handoff *is* the approval; a receiver who cannot approve must say so **before** accepting.
- **Descend-via into M98 requires a procedural altitude in the data block (7200.1O 3.8.D)** showing the published altitude at the M98 boundary fix. The order's own example: an aircraft on the KKILR arrival issued "descend via" carries a procedural altitude of **100**.
- **The interim-altitude waiver is six named sector pairs and no others (7200.1O 3.3.B.2):** **06→16 · 07→17 · 08→18 · 09→11 or 19 · 10→11 · 21→15**. Outside those pairs the receiving controller must not remove an interim altitude until communications are established *and* a different altitude has been issued. Non-Mode-C aircraft are excluded entirely (7110.65 5-14-4). **ZMP low altitude terminal sectors must not clear M98 departures above FL230 without prior verbal coordination** — same paragraph, and it is the sharpest M98-facing rule in the order.
- **Any route change requires a full route clearance (7200.1O 3.5).** If the pilot is unable, the fallback is radar vectors to a fix within vZMP airspace, then as filed.
- **vZMP combines at Sector 11, primary frequency 133.4 (7200.1O Appendix 03).** This is the en route equivalent of M98 collapsing to a single controller, and it carries a client trap the order calls out: in AFV, TX/RX must be selected for **MSP_CTR** or transceiver coverage is Sector 11's own airspace only, not the whole centre. Sector 11 is a high sector (`228B999`) with a 150 NM range limit.
- **Transfer-of-control releases across the boundary are per-neighbour, not uniform** (7200.1O Sector 01 §4.1.4.5, and the equivalent paragraph on each sector page): **ZMP/ZOB and ZMP/YWG** — receiving ARTCC may take beacon code changes and turns **up to 20°** within **20 miles** of the common boundary crossing point. **ZMP/YYZ** — **up to 45°** within 20 miles. **ZMP/APN** — APN has control for turns, climbs and descent on completion of handoff and communications transfer. **ZMP/AZO** — both facilities have control for turns up to **30°**, descent and code change within **10 miles** of the common boundary. **Each Area 1 low sector releases to every other Area 1 low sector** for descent and turns up to 20°.
- **Advance approach information for perimeter airports is assigned sector by sector (7200.1O Figure 3-1)** — a 33-row airport-to-sector table, transcribed in `claude_ZMP_SOP_Reference.md`. Sectors also assume the responsibility for airports inside a part-time approach control when that facility is closed.
- **The position relief briefing checklist is mandatory (7200.1O 2.3, Appendix 01)** and is ten items ending in a nine-part *traffic current/pending* list — non-radar, point-outs, holding, primary targets with no data block, aircraft in restricted areas/MOAs/ATCAAs/IRs, released but not airborne, handed off but still in the sector, VFR advisory. That list is the closest thing the order has to a published scan, and it is the natural spine for a ZMP drill debrief.
- **Three event splits are published (7200.1O Chapter 10):** MSP/M98 three-way, MSP/M98 with heavy KKILR/MUSCL traffic, and OMA/LNK focus. The prose for each is text; **every split diagram is a picture and is UNREAD.** The order also states the day-to-day preference plainly: open an underlying TRACON or local position before splitting vZMP airspace.
- **ZMP's neighbours are six centres and one FIR** — ZAU (Chicago), ZKC (Kansas City), ZDV (Denver), ZLC (Salt Lake City), ZOB (Cleveland), CZWG (Winnipeg FIR), plus **Toronto Centre (YYZ)**, which requires manual radar handoffs. All six centre LOAs are in `source-docs/` and extracted in `claude_ZMP_Enroute_LOA_Reference.md`.
- **ZMP's terminal neighbours are ten approach controls with their own LOAs** — M98, R90, RST, DSM, GRB, DLH, BIS, FAR, SUX, FSD — plus the minor approach controls in the ZMP Minor TRACON Reference. Master table in `claude_ZMP_TRACON_Interface_Reference.md`. **The Minor TRACON Reference is dated 2015-01-01 and is the oldest document in the set** — treat it as a working default the way the M98 project treats RST's hours.
- **The sector-to-neighbour adjacency map comes from vNAS, not from the SOP** (125 route-based coordination records in the facility JSON). It is the only machine-readable statement of which ZMP sector coordinates with which facility: **26, 27, 37 → R90** · **05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10 → M98** · **05, 07, 21, 36 → RST** · **12, 02, 01 → ZOB** · **04, 05, 12, 13, 15, 16, 17, 30, 36, 38 → ZAU** · **20, 23, 29, 39 → ZDV** · **27, 38, 39 → ZKC** · **20, 23 → ZLC** · **25 → CZWG**. **It is a coordination map, not a boundary map, and it is demonstrably incomplete** — Sectors 16, 17, 30 and 36 carry substantial M98 procedures in 7200.1O and appear in none of the M98 records. Absence from this map proves nothing. Full record set and method in `claude_ZMP_Sector_Reference.md`.
- **The M98 interface is layered, and "faces M98" is not a property of a sector on its own** (`claude_ZMP_Sector_Reference.md`, *The M98 interface*). The low tier is **05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 10**; the high tier feeding them from FL240 is **16, 17, 30, 36**. **Sector 10 is the only sector in the order whose narrative states a shared common boundary with M98 in words.** **Sector 05 is delegated the ZMP/M98 LOA Attachment A airspace 7,000 MSL and below, underlying the Sector 06 shelf** — 05 and 06 are stacked, not side by side. **Sector 06 is normally combined at Sector 16**, so the M98-arrivals-from-the-east job is often worked on 133.75 rather than 134.3. **Sectors 05 and 09 are additionally named in the Chapter 10 MSP/M98 event splits**, covering Sector 12's and Sector 18's airspace respectively.
- **The training environment split is a third configuration, owner-supplied 2026-09-01 and not in 7200.1O** (`claude_ZMP_Sector_Reference.md`, *The training environment split*). **Three seats partition the whole Area 2 + Area 3 low tier: seat 05 owns 05, 06, 21 · seat 09 owns 07, 08 · seat 10 owns 09, 10.** **Sector 09 is the odd one — you sit it but you do not own its airspace; seat 10 has it, and what you work from 09 is the rest of its area.** The three bundles cover exactly the seven low sectors of Areas 2 and 3, no sector twice, none missed — the smoke test asserts that partition. **This is what the M98 drill log's references to "sectors 5, 9 and 10" come from**, and it is not the order's single-scope guidance, which combines Area 2 at 05 and Area 3 at 07/18/09 and never moves 09's airspace to 10.
- **"Sector" is therefore ambiguous three ways and a drill frame must disambiguate all three:** which sector's *airspace* versus which *seat*; standing role versus Chapter 10 event role; and which *stratum*, since 06 is normally combined at 16.
- **M98's departures are organised into eight gates, and a gate consists of several SIDs** — owner-supplied 2026-09-01 and confirmed in **M98 7110.26A**, which gives the table as the first letter of scratchpad #1: **E** EAU · **D** DLL · **N** ODI · **B** BRD/DLH · **R** RST · **F** FAR · **A** ABR · **O** ONL/FOD. The scratchpad carries the gate letter plus the two-digit requested altitude — the order's example is **`B12`** for the BRD/DLH gate at 12,000. **This is a better basis for the SID-to-ZMP-sector question than the heading-band inference `../M98 Training/CLAUDE.md` currently records as unresolved**, and it should be carried back there. Detail and the corrections it forced in `claude_ZMP_Sector_Reference.md`.
- **The facility IDS carries a public, machine-readable split feed** (`claude_ZMP_IDS_Reference.md`). `/ids` itself is login-gated (403), but **`/api/eventsplits` answers publicly** with position-keyed sector bundles — the same data model as `TRAINING_SPLIT`, and the same *kind* of information Chapter 10 publishes only as pictures. Captured snapshot in `../_vnas/zmp_eventsplits.json`. **The IDS loads d3.js, so the authenticated side plausibly ships sector geometry — the best remaining lead on the boundary problem.**
- **Sector boundaries themselves are drawn, not written.** Every `Sector NN Map` page in 7200.1O is a picture; `ZMP_High_Sectors.pdf` and `ZMP_Approach_Sectors.pdf` have no text layer at all; `ZMP_Low_Sectors.pdf` yields ~56 KB of scattered chart labels from which sector numbers and altitude strata are recoverable but **positional association is not**. This is the single largest gap in the reference set and it is the one thing the owner has offered to supply as points. See `claude_ZMP_Gap_Analysis.md`.

## Architecture rules (non-negotiable)

Carried over verbatim in intent from `../M98 Training/CLAUDE.md`, with the namespace rule changed.

1. **Single file, no build step, no dependencies, offline-first.** Vanilla JS; the Google Fonts link is the only external reference in the markup and the page is fully functional without it. Any network call must be optional by construction, with a paste path that always works.
2. **Additive extension pattern.** Every `<script>` in the page is an extension block, in dependency order: Sector Deck, then Drill Builder, then the Handoff ID resolver. New features go inside an existing block or as a new one after them. The markup outside those blocks is chrome — topbar, hero, empty `#modnav`, footer — and the blocks mount everything else.
3. **One copy of every operational number, and the namespace is `ZD`.** The Sector Deck exposes `window.ZD`; the builder reads `window.ZD` and returns early if it is absent. **Do not use `DD` here.** M98 owns `DD`, and the two decks are never on the same page — the distinct namespace is what guarantees a paste-merge can never silently bind a terminal engine to en route data. Downstream blocks read geometry and reference values through `ZD` and never keep a second copy.
4. **Design system:** the M98 CSS custom properties verbatim (`--bg --panel --panel2 --ink --dim --dim2 --line --green --amber --red`, `--display --body --mono`). New CSS classes are namespaced `zd-`. Match the dark radar aesthetic; don't introduce new fonts or accent colors.
5. **Interface language:** full terminology, sentence case, no new abbreviations. Known domain abbreviations (ARTCC, ERAM, AIT, LOA, SOP, STAR, MIT, PRB, sector numbers) are fine.
6. Any spoken/TTS-string feature follows `claude_ZMP_TTS_Voicing.md` and `claude_US_Carrier_Callsigns.md` exactly. The owner listens to drill responses through text-to-speech, so written form is spoken form.
7. **En route data only.** Nothing MSP-terminal-specific crosses over from `../M98 Training/`: no gates, no feeder split, no near/far, no DDA, no ACDA, no configuration-driven floors, no runway assignment model. Those are properties of a TRACON. What *does* transfer is the architecture, the coordination reference (7110.65 Ch 5 Sec 4 is national), the callsign and TTS voicing docs, and the house voice.

## Roadmap (owner-prioritized)

1. **Sector boundaries.** Everything else is blocked on geometry. The SOP maps, the high-sector chart and the approach-sector chart are all pictures, and vNAS does not expose video-map GeoJSON (probed 2026-09-01; every candidate endpoint 404s). The path forward the M98 project used is the one available here: **owner-supplied points**, the way the M98 lateral boundary came in as nine MSP radial/DME pairs. Start with the sectors that matter — Area 2 and Area 3 around M98, and 26/27/37 around R90.
2. **A traffic model that is actually en route.** Not the M98 model with the gates removed. En route traffic is route-and-altitude over a large sector with LOA-governed boundary handoffs; the drill is *deliver the aircraft at the altitude and on the route the agreement requires*, and the grading spine is the LOA tables in `claude_ZMP_Enroute_LOA_Reference.md` and `claude_ZMP_TRACON_Interface_Reference.md`.
3. **AIT and Data Block Coordination as gradeable objects.** These are the two most distinctive ZMP procedures and both are fully written down with hard, checkable conditions (5 minutes, 3 minutes, Mode C, departure exclusion). They are the best first grading target in the whole facility.
4. **First OJT session.** Until one is flown, `claude_ZMP_OJT_Session_Review.md` stays a template and `STANDING` stays empty.
5. Descend-via / procedural-altitude drilling into M98, using the 7200.1O 3.8.D rule and the knock-it-off crossing altitudes in the ZMP-M98 LOA.
6. The three Chapter 10 event splits, once boundaries exist.
