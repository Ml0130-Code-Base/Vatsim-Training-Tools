# The master fly and drill blocks - M98's "Fly it" and "Set up a drill" pages, for every other deck

`fly-block.html` and `drill-block.html` at the repository root are the masters. Together they give a deck the two M98 pages the owner asked to have everywhere: **Set up a drill** (a brief, a seeded generator, a per-strip "flown on" panel, saved drills, JSON) and **Fly it** (a live clock, a scope, strips, a transmit box, a frequency feed, response timing, a debrief). Written 2026-10-27 on the owner's instruction: *"go through all facilities besides M98 and make sure they all have the same feel like M98 on the Set up a drill and fly it pages"*, with the scope settled as *"option 2 plus check if some of these inconsistencies are stemming from master blocks not being updated or current, go ahead and update the relative master block."* Tracked as issue #76.

**They are wired into the three ZID decks (IND, SDF, CVG), R90, Big Sky, C90, AZO and S56. They are not in M98, which keeps its own engine and builder, and not in ZMP Center, which is en route.**

## 1. What the owner decided, and what it waives

Option 2 was interface parity **plus a shared engine that flies each strip along its facility's CIFP published ladder**: the clock, the published crossings and the published speeds. That waives one sentence of the root `CLAUDE.md` section 3, *the jump from Tier 1 to Tier 2 is gated on geometry and on a first OJT session*, for the decks that carry the masters, and only for what CIFP itself publishes. Everything else in the contract holds:

- **Nothing spatial beyond the published track is claimed.** No boundary is crossed, no sector owns anything, no handoff receiver is right or wrong. A transmission to a frequency is read back and the aircraft goes; whether that was the correct position is not graded, because no deck carries the delegation that would say (invariant 3).
- **`STANDING` stays `[]`.** Nothing the engine flags is tagged to a standing item and the debrief says so in words (invariant 4).
- **Every check that is weaker than it looks says *"Reminder, not a judgement."***
- **No second network call** (invariant 6). Neither master contains `fetch`, `XMLHttpRequest`, `WebSocket`, `EventSource` or `sendBeacon`; the smoke test asserts it.

## 2. What flies, and what it flies on

| Strip | Flies | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Arrival | its STAR: transition, common route, runway transition | the deck's procedures block - `PFIX`, `STARS`, `procOf`, `trackTo` - read, never copied (invariant 5) |
| Departure | its SID: runway transition, common route, transition | the same |
| Overflight | the straight line from its entry radial/distance to its exit radial/distance | the strip's own boxes; refused by name if any is empty |

An arrival **descends via** the published altitudes and flies the published speeds until it is told something else. It keeps the 250 knot limit below 10,000. A departure climbs to the highest published floor ahead of it, or to the cleared altitude the drill gave it (`climbTo`), and holds there. A heading takes the aircraft off its published track (`via` goes false and it flies the heading); an assigned altitude or speed replaces the published one.

**What it does not do, and says in the feed:**

- No approach intercept, no final approach course, no threshold. A clearance is read back and the aircraft is handed to the tower.
- No wind. Heading and track are the same number.
- **Straight between fixes.** Arcs, holds and procedure turns are not flown, and a vector leg ends the published track.
- A procedure with fewer than two coordinates for the runway and transition chosen is **refused, strip by strip, with the reason**, not flown from a guess.
- **A radar-vector SID (ARINC 424 route type T; SLC4 and BOI3 are the two our fields code, MSP9 is not in CIFP at all) has no fix, so it has no published track.** `vecLegText` says what CIFP does code - the course and altitude of each CA, VM, FM, VA leg, carried as `crs` on vector SIDs only by `tools/cifp/gen.pl` (2026-10-27) - and the player flies a **straight stand-in line from the field to a charted fix on the published procedures at that field**: where its other SIDs leave the terminal area, or where its STARs begin, never a fix within 5 NM of the field. The strip names the fix (the drill builder's *vectored to* select; the engine picks the nearest), the feed says it is a stand-in, and which fix a real route uses is not published. A deck needs an `airportLL` for the field to start the line.

**Engine parameters are not aviation data.** `CLIMB` (jet 2400, turboprop 1500, piston 700 feet a minute), `DESC` (2000, 1200, 700), `ACCEL` (1.5 knots a second), `GRAD` (318 feet a nautical mile, the ceiling on a descent gradient, further limited to what the aircraft's descent rate can fly at its ground speed) and `TURN` (3 degrees a second) set how fast the scope moves. They are written as such in the block, they are not cited because they are not facts about the facility, and **they are exported as `fly.CLIMB`, `fly.DESC`, `fly.ACCEL`, `fly.GRAD` so a test can read them rather than copy them.**

**The transmit box** takes several instructions in one transmission, as a real one does: callsign, altitude (`descend and maintain 11000`), speed (`reduce speed to 250`), heading, direct, `expect runway`, `descend via`, a cleared approach (read back only), squawk, radar contact, ident, and `contact <frequency>`. Spoken digits are read as the number they are (`despell`: *one one thousand* is 11000, never 110). A bare `maintain` far from the current altitude is queried, not taken. A frequency belonging to the position you are working is refused; a frequency no position in the deck is on is refused and says so; an open position's frequency hands the aircraft off.

## 3. The fly contract

```js
window.FLY_FACILITY = {
  id:      'IND',            // facility label for every message
  prefix:  'indfly',         // id/class prefix and the stem of every global the block defines
  ns:      'IND',            // the deck's namespace; PFIX, procOf, trackTo for flown procedures
  host:    function(){},     // the element the player fills, or null
  airportLL: function(icao){},   // [lat, lon] from the deck's data
  // optional - the honest fallback is to leave the line out, never to guess
  ref, outline, flow, frame, positions, towerFor, callName, spoken, radiusNm, bank, cycle
};
```

`flow()` returns `{airport, land, dep, name}` and is what the generator reads, so **the runway comes from the configuration in force**. It may also carry `only:true`, which keeps the generator to the one field it names (Big Sky's two areas share no airspace, so a Boise drill carries no Bozeman traffic); without it a drill leans toward that field 80% of the time and draws on the others too. `positions()` returns `[{id, name, freq, open, owner, kind, self}]`. `bank(entry)` receives the debrief. `cycle` is quoted beside a published crossing (`CIFP 261001`) so the citation travels with the number.

`towerFor` is omitted at IND because the deck holds no tower or runway-ownership data to answer it. Without it the *contact tower* quick button is left out and "tower" is not an addressable receiver by name; a tower position the deck lists in `positions()` still receives by its own frequency.

**Exports.** `window.fly = {FACILITY, sim, load, validate, step, transmit, mk, track, viaTarget, parseAlt, despell, pointAt, xy, render, setRate, end, tracksFor, LAYER_MODES, transOptions, procFor, fixCum, conText, esc, mountCss, haveProcs, find, preview, CLIMB, DESC, ACCEL, GRAD}` and the handlers `window[prefix + 'Load' | 'Rate' | 'End' | 'Bank' | 'Send' | 'Quick' | 'Layers' | 'Range' | 'Pick' | 'Target']`.

A **drill** is `{id, name, brief, frame:{text}, strips:[strip...], rate:0|1|4|'hot', events:[event...]}`. `fly.load` refuses a drill the engine cannot fly and says why strip by strip; `fly.validate` is the same check without loading, and it names, as warnings, each event it will leave out. `brief` is stated in the feed, as the coach, when the drill loads.

**Events (2026-10-27, issue #76).** Two kinds, each fired once into the feed when the clock reaches `at` (seconds from the start; the clock is the player's own, so a paused drill fires nothing):

- `{kind:'speci', at, ceil, vis, wind, cat, cfg, cfgName}` - a special observation. Every field is optional text the drill's author typed; the feed prints what was set and nothing it was not. An event that sets none of them is left out. **With `cfg` it states that the configuration is now `cfgName` and that the player does not re-plan strips already flying**: the strips keep their published tracks, and the trainee re-vectors or re-assigns any that need it. The runways a generator chose are not recomputed, because that would be the engine asserting a re-plan it does not know how to make.
- `{kind:'landline', at, from, text, cs}` - a call from the other side. `from` and `text` are the author's own words; the block carries no catalogue of coordination calls because no deck transcribes the paragraphs that would define one. An event with no `text` is left out. The player expects no reply.

Neither is graded, neither is tagged to a standing item, and the debrief still says nothing was scored. They are notices on the feed, in the same chips as the rest of the feed.

**`fly.preview(strips)`** draws the start positions of a list of strips with `scopeSvg()` itself, so the geometry is the live scope's by construction: same fit, same rings, same outline, same tracks. It swaps a temporary `sim` into the module variable for the call and puts the live one back, strips every `onclick` and the pointer cursor, relabels the scope *Start positions*, and dims a strip that checks in later than the start. It returns `{svg, count, skipped}`; a strip with no flyable track has no position and is named in `skipped`. With no strips it returns an empty `svg`. **The scope origin and the outline are worked out again for every drill load and every preview (2026-10-28), and a preview leaves them alone while a drill is flying.** Before that they were kept from the first call, so a deck that moves its reference point between drills, as Big Sky does when you change area, drew the second area against the first's origin and outline.

## 4. The drill contract

```js
window.DRILL_FACILITY = {
  id:       'IND',
  prefix:   'inddrill',
  sectionClass: 'ind-section',          // optional: the deck's own heading class
  storeKey: 'ind-drill-deck-fly-v1',    // never a status word - the key outlives the status
  anchor:   function(){},               // the strips section: the builder opens above it, the save bar sits below
  rows:     function(next){},           // the strip block's rows; with an array, replace and re-render; always return the array
  page:     function(id){},             // optional: 'fly' / 'setup'
  mountRails: function(el){},           // optional: the notes rail, per section
  configs:  function(){},               // optional: [{id, name}] - the configurations or flows a special observation may name
  note:     'text',                     // optional: plain text for the Generate panel, saying what this deck's generator cannot build
  operators: {                          // optional (2026-10-28, issue #76): which operators fly each field
    KXXX: {src:'', flown:[{n:'Delta Air Lines', k:'passenger', cs:['DAL']}], named:[], gap:''}
  }
};
```

**`operators` is owner-supplied data, cited at the point of use.** A callsign at a field with an entry is drawn from `flown[]` and nowhere else: one brand, equal chance per brand, then one of the callsign designators that brand flies as (`cs`, from `claude_US_Carrier_Callsigns.md`; a regional brand such as American Eagle lists every operator that document assigns to it, because the sources never say which serves a given field). `named[]` is what the source names and the generator cannot fly (no designator, or not an airline) and `gap` is what the source does not hold; both are printed. **Equal chance is not a claim about traffic**: no source in the tree carries frequencies. A field with no entry keeps the flat list of twelve carriers and the *Operators at these fields* disclosure in the Generate panel says so field by field, so a gap is on the page and not found mid-drill. Overflights have no field and keep the flat list; the aircraft type is still drawn independently of the operator. Today the tables hold the airports the owner's eleven printouts cover (BOI, BZN, CVG, IND, MDW, OMA, ORD, PVU, SDF, SLC, and MSP as a record only); AZO has none. The tables and their sources are in `ZID/_shared/claude_ZID_Operators_Reference.md`, `ZLC/_shared/claude_ZLC_Operators_Reference.md`, `ZAU/C90/claude_C90_Operators_Reference.md` and `ZMP/R90/claude_R90_Operators_Reference.md`.

The frame and the configuration are read from `FLY_FACILITY` (`flow`, `frame`, `host`), so the deck states each of them once. **`configs` is the deck's own list, never the master's**: with it empty the *Special observation* panel offers no configuration to switch to. `note` replaces nothing the master says; it is where a deck states what it cannot draw (R90 and Big Sky have no coded SID to speak of, and Bozeman has no runway gate).

**What it mounts - M98's *Set up a drill* anatomy (2026-10-27, issue #76).** Under the position, a head section holds, in this order: **Generate** (seed, count, mix, *Generate drill*, *new seed*, *start blank instead*); **Edit** (name, opening rate, run hot, and the frame line the player will state); **Special observation - the frame changes mid-drill** (a time, and ceiling, visibility, wind, flight category and configuration to switch to, all typed); **Landline events - the other side of the operation** (any number of calls, each a time, a from, an optional callsign and what they say); **Brief - what the drill is asking of you**; then the strips section, which is the strip block's; then a tail section holding **Start positions - same geometry as the live scope** (`fly.preview` over the strips as they are now, repainted when a strip changes), **Flown on** (a per-strip transition select and a departure's cleared-altitude box), **Save and fly it** / **Save only**, **Saved drills** (*Fly it* / *Load into the strips* / *Delete*), JSON export and import, and a plain notice when storage is unavailable. It wraps `<stripprefix>Sync|Add|Drop` to repaint its details and follows the frame (a 1.5 second repaint plus any click or change on the page), so a deck's own seat and configuration controls need no listener of theirs. It never draws a strip; the row is the strip block's.

**What it does not carry, because the deck cannot say it.** M98's *What your notes want next* names the standing items a controller has been shown to need work on; `STANDING` is `[]` everywhere but M98 (invariant 4), so there is nothing to name and the panel is not built. M98's frame fields for the ATIS letter and the altimeter are not in the drill master either: each deck's Position Setup sets the frame its own data supports, and the SPECI event's text is the author's.

**Exports.** `window.drill = {store, generate, rng, collect, flyIt, KEY, storeOk, events, ui, refresh}` and the handlers `window[prefix + 'Set' | 'Gen' | 'Dice' | 'Blank' | 'AddLine' | 'DropLine' | 'Trans' | 'Climb' | 'SaveFly' | 'SaveOnly' | 'Fly' | 'Open' | 'Del' | 'Export' | 'Import']`. `drill.events()` is the events the panels currently describe, in the shape above.

### What the generator claims

Every procedure, runway and transition it picks is one the deck carries and the strip block accepts for that field. **Not aviation data, and each a box on the strip the trainee can change:** the altitude an arrival starts at, a departure's cleared altitude, the starting speed, the flight numbers (and the carrier at any field the deck names no operators for), the aircraft types and the stagger of the check-ins. They come from the fixture lists (`CARRIERS`, `JETS`, `PROPS`, `PISTONS`, `CLEARED`) at the top of the block. The generator is a seeded linear congruential generator (`s = (s * 1664525 + 1013904223) % 4294967296`), so **the same seed builds the same drill**, and the seed is in the box. **A kind the deck has no procedure for anywhere is drawn as an overflight in the mixed draw** (2026-10-27, found at AZO, where CIFP codes no SID or STAR at any field and a draw of six used to build one strip): the draw consumes the same random numbers either way, so a deck with both kinds builds the drill it always did.

What it guarantees beyond legality, because a drill that misses its own crossing on the first minute teaches the wrong thing:

- An arrival starts 35 to 80 NM from the end of its track, **never below the next published floor ahead and never so high it cannot make the lowest ceiling ahead** at the descent gradient the aircraft can fly.
- A departure is only generated where it can reach the highest SID floor ahead of it; otherwise it is swapped to a type that can, or skipped.
- The first two strips check in at once, the rest are staggered 30 to 150 seconds apart.

### The one gap in the strip, stated

**The strip carries no transition field.** The builder stores the choice as `trans` (and `climbTo` for a departure) on the row object, which the strip block's `readRow` mutates in place, so the properties survive. **A strip with no `trans` is resolved by `pickTrans`:** an arrival that names the fix it is running to (`dtgFix`) takes the transition that holds that fix, or none when the fix is in the common part; an arrival with no `dtgFix`, and every departure, takes the **first transition alphabetically**. That is a default, not a statement about what the facility assigns - CIFP does not say, and the SOP is where that lives. The chosen transition is `track.entry` on the built track. **No warning on the strip names the defaulted case, and the feed has not been checked to announce it**; a strip that matters should have its transition chosen in the *Flown on* panel.

## 5. Storage

The saved drills use their **own** key, `<basename>-fly-v1` (`ind-drill-deck-fly-v1`, `sdf-drill-deck-fly-v1`, `cvg-drill-deck-fly-v1`). The builder's key (`<basename>-v1`) holds the notes and the practice log; sharing it would put a corrupt drill list in the way of the trainee's own writing (issue #70). No status word appears in either. Where storage is unavailable the tool works in memory and **says so on the page**; the JSON export is the real backup.

## 6. Adopting it at another facility

1. **The strip block and the procedures block first.** Without a procedures block only overflights fly, and the page says so.
2. **Expose the strip rows.** In the strip mount, after `<prefix>RowAt`: `window[P + 'Rows'] = function(next){ if(next && next.length){ rows = next; render(); } return rows; };`
3. **Mark the strips flown.** In the procedures block's strip contract, `flies:true` for the procedures the fly block can fly (`track:true` is the separate claim that the ladder is carried, and the row labels the two differently).
4. **Give the Fly page a host.** Replace the *"No traffic model yet"* placeholder with a lede and `<div id="<prefix>fly-host">` holding the honest fallback text, so a deck where the block fails to load still states why there is no sector-level model.
   **Then keep the Fly page to the player, as M98's is.** M98's *Fly it* is one panel, the live player; an adopting deck's is too (owner, 2026-10-27, issue #76: the pages have to flow the same to a user, even though every section is derived from a master). A lateral-footprint plot or a published-procedures viewer the deck had there moves to the third page, the viewer beside the footprint where there is one, and the weather and D-ATIS reader sits on *Set up a drill* ahead of the builder it feeds. *Set up a drill* itself reads as M98's does, the position first, then the weather, then the builder: the reference tables a deck's Set up carried (the ZID decks' positions and fields tables) move to the third page. The sections keep their ids, so nothing that looks one up notices. Change the page lists, and the mount line of any block that appends itself to the Fly page.
5. **Add two blocks after the last block, each a contract followed by the master byte for byte**, and renumber the "Block N of M" headers. Splice before the **last** `</body>`.
6. **In the smoke test**, raise `EXPECTED_BLOCKS` and add section 11 (below).

### The checks section 11 carries (ZID smoke tests)

The exports exist; the contract names this facility and its own storage key, never the builder's; neither master contains a network call; `STANDING` is empty; **every published procedure builds a track out of the procedure table's own coordinates, in order, or is refused with a reason**; an 80 NM overflight is 80 NM; an overflight with a box empty is refused by name; an altitude is read the way the strip box says it (hundreds under 1,000, FL, commas, 0 is none); spoken digits read as the number they are; the same seed builds the same drill and a different seed a different one; **every generated strip is legal for the facility and every generated drill can be flown**; a drill the engine cannot fly is refused; **an untouched generated drill never misses a published crossing or speed limit** (eight seeds, 2,400 simulated seconds each); the transmit box takes altitude and speed in one transmission, takes a heading off the track, queries a far bare maintain, refuses a switch to your own position, hands off to an open position by its own frequency, and refuses a frequency no position is on; and **the debrief states that nothing is scored against a standing item.**

**Section 12 (2026-10-27, the Set up page's anatomy)** adds: the builder block alone mounts *Position Setup*, with M98's four sub-panels by name; the drill builder carries *Special observation*, *Landline events*, *Brief* and *Start positions* by name; the contract's `configs()` is the deck's own list; a drill nobody edited schedules no event; a drill with seven events, three of which say something, loads, names the four it leaves out and schedules three; nothing fires before its time, a SPECI fires at its time carrying exactly what the author typed, a landline fires from the side the author named, a configuration SPECI says the player does not re-plan, each event fires once; no event text claims a standing item; `fly.preview` draws one target per flyable strip and names the rest, returns the live scope's own svg relabelled with nothing clickable, leaves the loaded drill and its feed alone, and draws nothing for no strips. R90's copy also asserts the contract's `note`.

Run on 2026-10-27 through the browser runner (there is no Node on the owner's machine - root `CLAUDE.md` section 12, so `node smoke-test.mjs` itself has not been run): **IND 294 checks, SDF 308, CVG 386, R90 148, all passing** (276, 290, 368 and 129 before section 12). Separate untouched sweeps, many more seeds than the harness carries, flew CVG 640 strips and SDF 960 with **zero** missed published crossings or limits.

**What the sweep does not say.** A do-nothing run still produces losses of separation, orphaned aircraft and outline flags, and those are legitimate consequences of leaving traffic alone, not engine faults. The zero is about *published crossings and limits the engine itself should have met*, and nothing wider.

## 7. Drift

Both masters are byte-identical in every deck that carries them. Check it rather than assume it:

```bash
perl -0e 'local $/; open(D,"<:raw",$ARGV[0]); $t=<D>; open(M,"<:raw",$ARGV[1]); $b=<M>; $t=~s/\r\n/\n/g; $b=~s/\r\n/\n/g; $b=~s/\A<script>\n//; $b=~s/\n<\/script>\n\z//; print((index($t,$b)>=0?"identical":"DIFFERS"),"\n")' ZID/IND/ind-drill-deck.html fly-block.html
```

A change to a master lands in every copy in the same commit **within one ARTCC**; a deck's block is its contract followed by that body, so replace the body, never the contract. The decks of other ARTCCs catch up in their own commits, and until they do they carry no copy at all (nothing is out of step that is not there).

## 8. What is deliberately not here

- **M98's Position Setup panel is not a master.** Each adopting deck builds the same four sub-panels - *Quick setups*, *Which seat are you working*, *Open around you*, *What that resolves to* - out of its own data, because what a deck can honestly say differs: the ZID decks have a combining chain and offer *every radar position open* and *only this seat open* (their configuration and weather sit in a fifth sub-panel, *Configuration and weather*, which M98 sets in its Edit panel); R90 cannot say who is open (the combining table is a picture, issue #30), so its quick setups set the seat or the flow and nothing else; Big Sky's presets come from its own sector chains. Folding the four into a position master is a later piece of work, and a deck that cannot say what is open should keep saying so.
- **ZMP Center** is en route and flies overflights and direct only; it has no strips block, no procedures block and no cited scope reference point.
- **CIFP 261029 rolls on 2026-10-29** (issue #65). A procedure table is regenerated, never patched (root `CLAUDE.md` section 11), and the fly block reads whatever cycle the table carries.

## 9. Where a deck's contract differs from ZID's, and why

- **R90 (2026-10-27).** The builder has a seat and a north/south flow and nothing else, so the contract reads the flow from `rdb.state().flow` and takes the runways from `DEP_HEADINGS.OMA` (OMA 7110.4A 5.c.4). There is **no outline** (R90 has no boundary and the SimAware footprint is deliberately not wired in), **no `callName`** (the radio name is not stated in the R90 documents, so the feed uses the bare facility id), **no `bank`** (the builder has no debrief box), and `positions()` offers all six R90 positions as staffed because the combining table is a picture (issue #30); nothing is graded about the choice. KOMA and Lincoln have **no coded SID**, so the generator skips departures and says so, and the strip contract's `flies:true` covers the five STARs only. The builder's old *Build a drill* placeholder is gone (the drill master is the builder now) and its sentence about what the generator cannot build is the drill contract's `note`; the contract's `configs()` is the two flows. *Position Setup* holds the four sub-panels, with *Quick setups* setting the seat or the flow through the selects' own change handlers.
- **Fallback route layers.** A deck without the route-draw master gets the fly block's own *all STARs / all SIDs* layers, which draw each entry transition, the common route and each runway transition as separate lines (changed 2026-10-27: it used to draw only the common route, so MARWI4 and LANTK2 at KOMA were missing). A deck with the route-draw master uses that instead.
- **Big Sky (2026-10-27).** Both blocks sit **before** the pages block, which must stay last, and that block now lists the player's section (`mod-bskfly`) alone under *Fly it* and the drill builder's sections around the strips under *Set up a drill* (the strips moved there from *Fly it*, as at the ZID decks), with the weather and D-ATIS panel (`mod-bskw`) beside *Area and flow* on *Set up a drill* and the published procedures (`mod-bskp`) on the third page. *Position Setup* (`mod-pos`) is the four sub-panels, with the seat's vertical structure and top-down table (`mod-seat`, reference rather than setup) moved to the third page; the contract's `configs()` is `ZLC.flowsFor` of the area picked and its `note` says what the generator cannot build. The flow is the BOI runway gate (`KBOI 10L/R` is 10L and 10R, `KBOI 28L/R` is 28L and 28R - BOI 7110.2B appendices 1B and 1C, carried in `FLOWS`); **Bozeman's blocks carry no gate, so a Bozeman drill has no flow and its strips carry no runway.** The flow sets `only:true`, because the two areas share no airspace and a Boise drill must carry no Bozeman traffic. The scope origin and an overflight's radial and distance are measured from the BOI or BZN navaid CIFP carries, **not the airport reference point**, and a departure starts at its first coded fix because no field coordinate is carried. BOI3 is a vector SID with no fix and is refused by name. The deck has **no smoke test** (issue #46); until it has, the fly and drill blocks there are verified by driving the real page, not by a harness.
- **C90 (2026-10-27).** Both blocks are blocks 6 and 7, after the strips block. The legacy engine (`DD.sim.ac`, `mkAc`, `tick`, `transmit`, the scope, the strip list and the feed) is retired, because a second traffic model on one page contradicts the first; `DD.sim` keeps the frame (`cfg`, `mdw`, `open`, `seat`) and the contract reads `DD.frameText()`, `DD.flowNow()` and `DD.openSet()`. The flow is O'Hare's runways as the union of what each Final owns in the arrival configuration (4-3.b.1, written as CIFP writes a runway, `09L` not `9L`); a configuration states no departure runway, so none is claimed, and O'Hare and Midway have no coded SID. The outline is the VATGlasses footprint, one polygon to FL150 for every position, and the player does not read the lid. The reference point is the RRCP. *Lost with the engine:* the scope's remark that a strip is above FL150 and the bearing, distance and heading placement boxes. The feeder gate stays a label (issue #44).
- **AZO (2026-10-27).** Blocks 6 and 7, plus a small block 8 that re-homes the one check worth keeping. CIFP 261001 codes no SID and no STAR at any West Wall field, so **only overflights fly**: an arrival or a departure is refused by name, the strips block's two procedure lists are empty and the row says *frame only*, and the generator draws overflights (see the generator note above). Radials and distances are measured from the Kalamazoo field (`AZO0`, the VATGlasses airport coordinate this deck has always quoted from; the order cites no radar site). **There is no flow and no configuration**: the Great Lakes order names none and no West Wall field publishes a D-ATIS, so `flow()` returns null, `configs()` is empty and *Position Setup* says so in a *Configuration* panel rather than omitting it.
